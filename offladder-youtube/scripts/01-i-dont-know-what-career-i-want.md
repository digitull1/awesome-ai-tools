# Script 01 · I Don't Know What Career I Want

| | |
|---|---|
| **Series** | Honest Answers |
| **Target length** | 9:30 |
| **Central question** | What do I actually do when I don't know what career I want? |
| **Viewer** | Undecided Students (15–22) and the Quietly Stuck (27–45) |
| **Titles** | A: *I Don't Know What Career I Want (Try This Before Anything Else)* · B: *No Idea What Career You Want? Do This, Not a Test* · C: *How to Figure Out What Career You Want (Without Deciding Anything)* |
| **Thumbnail** | Archetype F: host at a kitchen table holding one handwritten sheet, looking into the lens. Orange box: **NO IDEA? GOOD.** |
| **Location** | A lived-in kitchen, not a studio. It signals honesty and matches where people actually have this worry. |
| **Props** | A blank A4 sheet, a pen, and the host's own completed sheet (real) for the demonstration |
| **Claims register** | No statistics. One host anecdote marked **[HOST: real]**. |

---

## [0:00–0:30] Cold open: the pause

**PICTURE:** Tight on the host at the kitchen table. A blank sheet and a pen in front of them.

**HOST:** "Someone asks what you want to do. You say, 'I'm not sure yet.' And then there's this pause."

**PICTURE:** Hold on the host for a second and a half. The room tone drops out completely.

**HOST:** "In that pause, you can feel them deciding you haven't got it together. I want to talk about that pause, because it's wrong. You're not behind. You're missing information. And in the next ten minutes, you're going to collect some."

**ON SCREEN:** YOU'RE NOT BEHIND. YOU'RE MISSING INFORMATION.

**HOST:** *(sliding the pen towards the lens)* "Grab a pen. You don't need to know what you want. That's kind of the point."

> **Retention:** The Pause (hook archetype 1) names a private feeling precisely enough that the viewer feels seen within five seconds. "Grab a pen" commits them to taking part before 0:30, and "ten minutes" is an honest promise of length.

---

## [0:30–1:45] Why thinking about it doesn't work

**HOST:** "Here's what's actually happening when you sit down and try to work out your future. You're asking your brain to imagine a job it has never done, and then predict how you'd feel doing it. We are really bad at that."

**PICTURE:** A card with a job title on it: ARCHITECT. Cut to a glossy imagined version (sketches, a glass building at sunset), then a hard cut to plain reality: a planning email, a spreadsheet of costs, a site visit in the rain. Film simple inserts or use illustration. No stock footage.

**HOST:** "We imagine the title. The salary. How it sounds when we say it at a party. We don't imagine the Tuesday afternoon: the actual texture of the hours. And the Tuesday afternoon is what decides whether you last."

**ON SCREEN:** YOU DON'T NEED A CAREER PLAN. YOU NEED A TUESDAY.

**HOST:** "It's entirely possible to spend two years deciding, commit, and then find out in six weeks what a single afternoon would have told you. So we're not going to decide anything today. We're going to find out. Four steps. Here's the first."

> **Retention:** The reframe is the "oh" moment. "You need a Tuesday" is written to be quoted, and it works as a Short on its own. "Four steps" gives the viewer a progress bar to hold on to.

---

## [1:45–3:15] Step 1: when did you lose track of time?

**ON SCREEN:** 1 / 4 · LOST TRACK OF TIME

**HOST:** "Step one. Write down three times in the last year you lost track of time. Not things you're good at. Not things that look good on a CV. Things where you looked up and somehow it was two hours later."

**ON SCREEN:** Pause card: **PAUSE. WRITE THREE THINGS.** A ten-second countdown runs for viewers who don't pause.

**HOST:** *(over the countdown)* "Anything counts. Rearranging your room. Arguing about a football match. Fixing a friend's laptop. Planning a trip nobody asked you to plan."

**PICTURE:** Top-down shot of the host writing their own list.

**HOST:** "Here's mine." **[HOST: real. Read your three genuine items. Specific beats impressive: "Re-cutting a video of my cousin's wedding until the timing felt right" is better than "creativity".]**

> **Retention:** The first participation beat. From here on, the video is about the viewer's own life. The host going first makes it feel safe.

---

## [3:15–5:00] Step 2: find the verb

**ON SCREEN:** 2 / 4 · FIND THE VERB

**HOST:** "Step two, and this is the bit that changes things. Job titles are a terrible way to think about work. 'Doctor' isn't one thing. It's diagnosis, paperwork, night shifts, delivering bad news, and thirty other things you might love or hate separately. What you actually spend your hours on is verbs. So take each thing on your list and push it down to the verb underneath."

**PICTURE:** Animated sheet. Each item slides left, an orange arrow draws, and the verb appears.

**HOST:** "Editing videos for hours becomes *arranging things until the timing feels right.* Arguing about football tactics becomes *finding the pattern that explains why something works.* Sorting out a friend's crisis: *hearing the real problem under the one they said.* Rebuilding a bike: *making a broken physical thing work again.*"

**ON SCREEN:** Each transformation lands as its own card, one every three or four seconds.

**HOST:** "Now do yours."

**ON SCREEN:** **PAUSE. ONE VERB PER LINE.** Ten-second countdown.

**HOST:** "Here's why this matters. Verbs travel. 'Finding the pattern that explains why something works' is sports analysis. It's epidemiology. It's fraud detection, product research, and work that doesn't have a settled name yet. You can't search for a job title you've never heard of. But you can search for the verb."

**ON SCREEN:** YOU CAN'T SEARCH FOR A JOB YOU'VE NEVER HEARD OF. YOU CAN SEARCH FOR THE VERB.

**HOST:** "Quick thing: every week on this channel we take a verb like that and test a real job with it. Not describe it. Test it. If that's useful, subscribe. Right. Step three."

> **Retention:** Four transformations in about 25 seconds are rapid micro-payoffs. The football-to-epidemiology leap is the surprise the thumbnail promised. The single subscribe line lands after the first payoff (about 50% of runtime) and is tied to the channel promise.

---

## [5:00–6:15] Step 3: widen before you narrow

**ON SCREEN:** 3 / 4 · WIDEN THE FIELD

**HOST:** "Step three. Before you choose anything, widen the field. Quick test. Name as many jobs as you can in ten seconds. Go."

**ON SCREEN:** A ten-second timer, with a ticking clock sound.

**HOST:** "How many did you get? Now, how many of those have you actually watched someone do, up close? If you're anything like me, it's the jobs my family did, my teachers, and whatever was on TV. That's the list you're choosing your future from."

**HOST:** "So for each verb, find three directions you'd never have thought of. Two ways to do it. One: ask someone who works near something you like, 'What jobs exist near yours that nobody applies for?' Two: open a job board for an industry you know nothing about and write down every role you can't even decode."

**PICTURE:** Screen recording of a job board in an unfamiliar industry, with baffling job titles highlighted in orange. Blur company names.

**HOST:** "Weird and unfamiliar is the point. At this stage you want coverage, not a shortlist. A lot of 'I don't know what I want' is really 'I haven't met enough of what exists.'"

> **Retention:** The Viewer Challenge (hook archetype 10) lets the viewer produce the evidence themselves, so we make the point without claiming a statistic. The job-board screen recording is an immediate, copyable method.

---

## [6:15–7:45] Step 4: test three, cheaply

**ON SCREEN:** 4 / 4 · TEST THREE

**HOST:** "Step four. Pick three directions and do one small, real thing for each. Small means you can finish it this month, it costs nothing, you don't need anyone's permission, and at the end there's something outside your own head."

**ON SCREEN:** Checklist: FINISHABLE THIS MONTH · FREE · NO PERMISSION NEEDED · LEAVES EVIDENCE

**HOST:** "Three kinds of test work for almost anything. One: a conversation. Ask someone doing the work what last Tuesday looked like, hour by hour, and which part they'd delete if they could. Two: a miniature. Not a course about the work. The work. Write the one-page report. Take the photos. Build the tiny model. Three: proximity. Spend half a day near it. Volunteer, help out, sit in."

**PICTURE:** A quick montage for each type. At launch use simple inserts; once the channel has episodes, use clips from 90 Minutes As… and Last Tuesday.

**HOST:** "If you're under sixteen, stick to the tests that don't need strangers. Make the miniature, or ask an adult you trust to introduce you to someone."

> **Retention:** Concrete and doable. The "which part they'd delete" question is intriguing enough that viewers will want the episodes built around it. The safety line matters because a 13-year-old may be watching.

---

## [7:45–8:30] How to read what happened

**HOST:** "Afterwards, don't ask yourself 'did I enjoy it?' That's too blunt. Ask three things. Which twenty minutes went fastest? What did I put off? Would I do it again next week without anyone asking?"

**ON SCREEN:** The Scorecard graphic. Each question ticks as it's read.

**HOST:** "And if one of them bores you? Brilliant. That's a real result. You've just ruled something out for the price of an evening instead of three years. Write down exactly which part bored you. That detail usually points at the direction next door."

> **Retention:** The first appearance of the Scorecard, the channel's signature device. Permission to fail is a relief, and relief keeps people watching.

---

**HOST:** "So here's your week. Tonight: your three lost-track-of-time moments, and their verbs. By the weekend: three directions you'd never heard of. Next week: one small test. Put it in your calendar as a real slot. A vague plan doesn't survive Wednesday."

**ON SCREEN:** A Your Week card with three lines: TONIGHT · BY THE WEEKEND · NEXT WEEK

**HOST:** "And if you'd rather have a couple of directions picked for you, plus one small thing to try this week, that's what we built OffLadder for. It's three questions at offladder.com. It's free, there's no account, and it takes about two minutes. It's a starting point to test, not a verdict. Same as everything on this channel."

**ON SCREEN:** Lower third: **OFFLADDER.COM · 3 QUESTIONS · FREE**

> **Retention:** Value first, then the ask. The viewer already holds a list of verbs, so the product arrives as the natural next step, not an interruption.

## [9:00–9:30] Callback and bridge

**HOST:** "And next time someone asks what you want to do? There's no pause. You say, 'I'm testing a couple of directions.' Which, by then, will be true."

**PICTURE:** The host slides the finished sheet towards the lens. Clean space opens on the right for the end screen.

**HOST:** "If you want to see what a test actually looks like, I gave myself ninety minutes to plan a festival, and then someone cancelled on me. It's right here."

**ON SCREEN:** End screen. Element 1: video 02. Element 2: the Honest Answers playlist. Element 3: subscribe.

> **Retention:** A callback to the opening makes the video feel complete. The bridge gives a specific reason to watch the next video instead of saying goodbye.
