## Pre-production

### The practitioner
A working verification professional, such as a newsroom verification journalist, an open-source investigator, or a trust and safety or digital forensics analyst. Get their employer's written permission, and agree in advance what can be shown about their tools and cases.

### The three guess rounds
We make every image ourselves, so no real person, event or news story is faked or misrepresented.

| Round | Image | Truth | Why it's in the video |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A real photo we took that *looks* fake (strange light, odd weather, an unlikely coincidence) | Real | Instinct isn't evidence |
| 2 | An AI-generated image we made of an innocuous scene (for example a market street), with no real person's likeness | AI-generated | The obvious fear |
| 3 | A real photo we took of a harmless scene (for example a crowded beach), given a false caption that moves it to a different day | Real photo, false context | Lets the practitioner explain the kind of case they actually see, in their own words |

**Labelling rules:** as soon as each round is revealed, the image carries an on-screen label (**AI-GENERATED FOR THIS VIDEO** or **DEMONSTRATION: FALSE CAPTION**) until it leaves the screen. Tick YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure when uploading ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)).

### Guardrails
- Show methods at the level a curious viewer can use: searching, reading and cross-checking. **Never show how to make fakes harder to detect.**
- A detector's score is a probability, not a verdict. Let the practitioner say this in their own words.

### Practitioner interview questions (capture)
1. "What do you actually do, in one sentence?"
2. "What did last Tuesday look like, hour by hour?"
3. "What kind of case takes up most of your time?" *(Don't prompt "AI fakes". Let them answer.)*
4. "What's the first thing you check, every time?"
5. "When do you say 'I don't know'?"
6. "Which part of the job would you delete?"
7. "How did you get into this? What do people usually come from?"
8. "What's changing because of AI, in both directions?"
9. "What does nobody know yet about the future of this work?"

### Claims register
| Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Content Credentials (the C2PA standard) can carry a record of how an image was made, where present | C2PA / Content Credentials documentation | **[FACT-CHECK]** |
| Many platforms strip metadata when images are uploaded | Practitioner, plus platform documentation | **[FACT-CHECK]** |
| Anything about how common a kind of case is | Practitioner's own experience, attributed to them, never presented as a statistic | Capture |

## [3:45–5:45] The method, in four questions

**HOST:** "So how do you actually do it? From watching [Name] work, it came down to four questions."

**ON SCREEN:** Four cards, one at a time, each with the practitioner demonstrating at a usable, non-technical level:

1. **WHERE DID IT FIRST APPEAR?** Reverse image search, and hunting for the earliest upload.
2. **DOES THE WORLD AGREE WITH THE PICTURE?** Signs, language, landmarks, weather and shadows checked against the claimed place and time.
3. **WHAT DOES THE FILE SAY?** Metadata where it survives, and Content Credentials where present. **[FACT-CHECK]**
4. **HOW SURE AM I, AND WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND?** The write-up.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** when they say "I don't know" (question 5).

**HOST:** "That fourth question is the actual job. Anyone can have a hunch. Being paid means writing down how sure you are, and why, in a way that holds up when someone checks."

**ON SCREEN:** A HUNCH ISN'T A FINDING.

**HOST:** "If this is the kind of thing you'd stay up late doing: every week on this channel we explore work like this, honestly. Subscribe and you'll catch the next one."

> **Retention:** A numbered method gives a sense of progress, and each card is a micro-payoff. The subscribe line lands after the method, the video's main payoff so far.

## [5:45–7:15] Round 3, and what AI changes

**PICTURE:** Round 3's photo, with its false caption displayed as a social post.

**VO:** "Last round. Is this post telling the truth?"

**ON SCREEN:** **TRUE OR FALSE? DECIDE NOW.** 3… 2… 1…

**HOST:** "{Real guess.}" **[HOST: real]**

**ON SCREEN:** Reveal: **REAL PHOTO. FALSE CAPTION. DEMONSTRATION MADE FOR THIS VIDEO.** The label stays on screen.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** their reaction and how they'd trace it, and, if they raise it themselves, how often their real cases involve context rather than fake pixels.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** what's changing because of AI, in both directions (question 8).

**ON SCREEN:** Two columns: **AI MAKES FAKES CHEAPER** / **AI HELPS CHECKERS SEARCH FASTER**. Fill them only with the practitioner's own points.

> **Retention:** The twist round. It isn't a trick image at all, which reframes the fear the viewer arrived with and pays off the thumbnail's question in an unexpected way.

## [8:15–9:15] What it grows out of

## [9:15–10:00] What nobody knows yet

**HOST:** "Here's the honest part. Nobody knows yet how this work settles."

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** what nobody knows yet (question 9).

**ON SCREEN:** WHAT'S GENUINELY UNKNOWN, as a list drawn from their answer. For example, whether detection keeps pace with generation, whether provenance standards spread, and what the job ends up being called.

> **Retention:** The honesty beat is the series signature. Ending the information section on uncertainty, not hype, is what makes viewers trust the next episode.
