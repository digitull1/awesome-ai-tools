## Why this series

Almost everyone, whatever their age or career stage, is anxious about what AI will do to work. In the US, 52% of workers say they're worried about the future impact of AI in the workplace ([Pew Research Center, February 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/02/25/u-s-workers-are-more-worried-than-hopeful-about-future-ai-use-in-the-workplace/)). The fear is specific: *the job I'm doing, or the course I'm about to choose, might not exist.*

That fear is the most searched, most shared tension in careers content, and it's exactly the problem OffLadder was built for: *"Built for a world where AI is changing jobs faster than traditional career advice can keep up"* (offladder.com). The series names the fear with real quotes and real numbers, then turns it into something the viewer can do, and shows how OffLadder helps.

**The promise:** *Every episode takes one real thing said about AI and work, shows what the evidence actually says, and turns it into a move you can make this week.*

## The format (30 seconds)

| Beat | Time | Job | Example (episode 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–4 s | A verbatim quote or figure, readable on the first frame, with speaker and date on screen | *"Probably none of us will have a job."* Elon Musk, May 2024 |
| **Proof** | 4–8 s | The number that makes it concrete, with the source on screen | WEF: employers expect 92 million jobs displaced and 170 million created by 2030 |
| **Twist** | 8–12 s | The work that's appearing, which nobody told you about | Eight emerging directions from offladder.com |
| **Approach** | 12–16 s | Nobody knows which jobs will last, so test before you bet | *"Nobody knows which jobs will last. Not even the people building AI."* |
| **OffLadder** | 16–24 s | The adaptive loop, shown with the site's own illustrative example | Explore → try → learn → adapt |
| **End** | 24–30 s | The line, the site, a comment prompt, and a seamless loop back to the hook | *"Don't pick your future. Test it."* |

It's typographic motion design, not a talking head: huge verbatim quotes on ink, data that animates literally (one square per million jobs), then a turn to light when the answer arrives. Sound is an original synthesised score cut to the same beat grid as the picture.

## Rules for this series

These keep a fear-driven format credible. They're not optional.

1. **Quotes are verbatim**, with speaker, venue and date on screen and the source in the description. Predictions are shown as someone's prediction, never as fact.
2. **Figures are the source's own**, with the source on screen. Employer-survey projections are labelled as what employers *expect*.
3. **No zombie statistics.** Never use "65% of children will do jobs that don't exist yet": it has no traceable source.
4. **No likenesses.** Text only: no photos or video of the people quoted, and nothing that implies they endorse OffLadder.
5. **The house rules still apply:** no AI-proof lists, no salary bait, nothing invented (see [01-strategy.md](../01-strategy.md)).
6. **OffLadder claims come from offladder.com's own copy**, and the example of the loop is labelled *illustrative*, as it is on the site.
7. **End with agency, not doom.** Every episode turns the fear into a next step.

**Why it works.** The first frame is a sentence from the world's most-watched tech figure, readable at thumbnail size, and it names the viewer's fear exactly. The second quote proves it isn't one person's hot take. The grid makes an abstract number physical, and the honest half of it (170 million new jobs) sets up the turn. The job cards create a curiosity gap, and "Not even the people building AI" converts fear into permission to stop guessing. The product appears as the answer to the question the video raised, not as an ad break.
