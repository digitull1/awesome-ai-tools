## Casting

Recruit through the channel's casting form and personal networks. **Never through OffLadder user data.**

**Look for someone who:**
- Is in work, and can give four to six hours a week for six weeks without putting that work at risk.
- Has real constraints (a mortgage or rent, dependants) and is willing to talk about them at a level of detail *they* choose.
- Is curious about a direction, or is willing to find one in week 1.
- Understands that "off the table" is a successful outcome, not a failure.
- Is **not** in acute financial or emotional crisis. This is a test, not a rescue, and filming someone in crisis isn't fair to them.
- Is allowed to take part under their employment contract and their employer's social media policy.

**Avoid:** anyone who seems to want to be filmed more than they want the answer. Viewers can tell.

---

## Duty of care and consent

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| Consent | A signed release at the start, and written re-confirmation before publishing. The subject can withdraw up to publication. |
| Review | The subject sees the fine cut for **factual accuracy and personal safety**. This is not editorial control, and we say so up front. |
| Employer | Not named or identifiable unless the subject chooses and the employer agrees. Blur badges, screens and logos. |
| Money | The subject decides what is shown: real figures, ranges, or just the conclusion ("close to my floor"). |
| Family | No children on camera. Partners appear only with their own signed release. |
| Wellbeing | A check-in at every shoot. Stop filming whenever asked. Signpost support (OffLadder's safe-use page points people to someone they trust, or a local service, if they feel overwhelmed). No pressure to produce a dramatic outcome. |
| Payment | Pay the subject a fair fee for their time, agreed up front and not tied to any outcome. |

## Filming schedule

## [0:00–0:40] Cold open: week 6

**PICTURE:** Week 6. The kitchen table. Their notes, crossed out and rewritten. They pick up a pen.

**SUBJECT (capture):** a real line from the decision session. Don't prompt it. *Expected shape:* "I've written it three times and crossed it out twice."

**VO (draft):** "Six weeks ago, [Name] said, 'I want out.' Today is the decision: more of this, a different version of it, or off the table. [Name] doesn't know which it's going to be. Neither do we."

**ON SCREEN:** The progress bar, all six segments full. Series title card, one second.

> **Retention:** The biggest open loop on the channel, held for twenty minutes. The Call-Out energy (hook archetype 9) is carried by a real face rather than a line to camera.

---

## [0:40–2:30] Who and why

**SUBJECT (capture), week 0 interview:**
1. "Tell me about a normal Tuesday in your job now."
2. "When did you first think, 'not twenty more years of this'?"
3. "What's good about the job? What would you miss?"
4. "Who depends on your income?" *(They choose how much to say.)*
5. "What are you most worried about?"
6. "What would 'this worked' look like in six weeks?"

**PICTURE:** Observational footage of their real routine (commute, desk, school run), always with anything identifying blurred.

**VO (draft):** "[Name] is [age]. [One-sentence situation, in their words.] Good at the job. And, in [their] own words, [real quote]."

> **Retention:** Identity. The viewer recognises the situation before the method arrives. Question 3 matters because the job not being all bad is what makes the decision real.

---

## [2:30–4:00] The arithmetic

**PICTURE:** A sheet of paper with three headings in the subject's handwriting: **MONTHLY FLOOR · MONTHS OF RUNWAY · YEAR-ONE PAY**.

**VO (draft):** "For [Name], the question wasn't courage. It was a number. So we started there."

**SUBJECT (capture):** working through the three figures, at the level of detail they've agreed.

**ON SCREEN:** Three outcomes, as OffLadder's method sets them out:
- Year-one pay **close to the floor**: move properly once one test has gone well.
- **Well below** the floor: the parallel track is the only version that works.
- **Can't find** the pay figure: that's a finding in itself, so ask two people doing the work.

**ON SCREEN:** An Evidence Card for any pay figure, from **live job adverts or real freelance rates**, with the date. **[FACT-CHECK]**

> **Retention:** The segment this audience almost never sees on YouTube, and the one they calculate privately at 2am. Realism is the retention device.

---

## [4:00–6:00] Week 1: the inventory

**ON SCREEN:** WEEK 1 · the progress bar fills one segment.

**SUBJECT (capture):** listing *"the three parts of my job I'm genuinely good at"*. Push for specifics ("untangling a messy process", not "problem solving").

**PICTURE:** Screen recording: searching for adverts built mostly from those three parts, in an industry they've never worked in. The moment they find one and read it closely.

**SUBJECT (capture):** which requirements they already meet. Film the reaction to the first "I already do that."

**VO (draft):** "The method at this age isn't reinvention. It's redeployment: taking twenty years of competence somewhere it's scarcer. Change one thing, the industry, the customer or the craft, not all three."

**ON SCREEN:** Four routes, one highlighted for this subject: **SAME CRAFT, NEW INDUSTRY · SAME INDUSTRY, NEW CRAFT · PRACTITIONER TO ENABLER · EMPLOYEE TO INTERIM**

> **Retention:** The first discovery. "I already do that" is a small, true victory the viewer shares.

---

## [6:00–8:30] Week 2: the conversation

**ON SCREEN:** WEEK 2

**PICTURE:** A twenty-minute conversation with someone doing the target work (their own release signed).

**SUBJECT asks (capture):**
1. "What's the boring part of your week?"
2. "Who hires for this, and what do they actually screen for?"
3. "What does the money look like at my level of experience?"
4. "What would you tell someone in my position?"

**PICTURE:** The drive or walk home, then diary camera that evening.

**VO (draft):** "Two conversations teach you more than two weeks of reading, because you can ask about the parts nobody writes down."

> **Retention:** A new voice resets attention halfway through the first act, and the answer to "the boring part" is often the episode's first real surprise.

---

## [8:30–13:00] Weeks 3–4: the real piece of work

**ON SCREEN:** WEEKS 3–4

**VO (draft):** "Nobody hires a plan. They hire someone who's already done a small piece of it. So for two weeks, [Name] does one."

**PICTURE:** The smallest real piece of the new work, for a **real recipient**: a paid or volunteer brief, a project with an actual user, a piece of work someone will use. Shot over two half days.

**[CAPTURE: the low point.]** It usually arrives in the diary camera, not on a shoot day. Ask permission before using it.

**SUBJECT (capture), after delivering:** the recipient's reaction, and the subject's reaction to that reaction.

> **Retention:** The Stakes Ladder rises from private to delivered to someone real. The low point creates empathy, and empathy is what keeps a 20-minute documentary watched to the end.

---

## [13:00–15:30] Week 5: what pulled, what dragged

**ON SCREEN:** WEEK 5

**PICTURE:** The subject reading their five weeks of two-sentence notes aloud. Handwritten, filmed top-down.

**SUBJECT (capture):** *"What pulled? What dragged? What surprised you?"*, looking across all five weeks.

**PICTURE:** Pay research from two live sources, on screen with dates.

**VO (draft):** "Five weeks of notes. Not a feeling about a job title: evidence about specific hours."

> **Retention:** The payoff of the method. The viewer sees the notes add up, and it's the moment before the decision, so tension is high.

---

## [15:30–18:00] Week 6: the decision

**ON SCREEN:** WEEK 6 · the progress bar is full.

**PICTURE:** Pick up exactly where the cold open left off: the kitchen table, the pen.

**SUBJECT (capture):** the written decision: **MORE**, **VARIANT** or **OFF THE TABLE**, with the written reason read aloud.

**SOUND:** Two seconds of silence before the stamp appears.

**ON SCREEN:** The orange verdict stamp.

**VO (draft, one version per outcome):**
- **More:** "More of this. Not a leap. The next test, a bigger one, with the day job still in place."
- **Variant:** "A different version. [What changes and why, in their words.]"
- **Off the table:** "Off the table, with a written reason. Six weeks, not six years. That's what the test is for."

> **Retention:** The loop from 0:00 closes. Every outcome is written as a success, because in this method it is one.

---

## [18:00–19:00] Epilogue

**PICTURE:** Three to six weeks later.

**SUBJECT (capture):** *"What's happened since? What would you tell yourself at week 0?"*

---

## [19:00–19:40] Your six weeks

**ON SCREEN:** The six-week template, one card per stage:
- **Week 1:** List three parts of your job you're genuinely good at. Find two adverts built mostly from them in an unfamiliar industry.
- **Week 2:** Have one twenty-minute conversation with someone doing that work. Ask about the boring part of the week.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Do the smallest real piece of it for a real recipient. Paid is better; public is enough.
- **Week 5:** Write what pulled and what dragged, then find year-one pay from two live sources.
- **Week 6:** Decide: more of this, a different variant, or off the table with a written reason.

**HOST or VO:** "If you'd like help with week one, the directions to test, that's what we built OffLadder for. Three questions at offladder.com. Free, no account."

**HOST or VO (casting):** "And if you're thinking about changing direction and you'd be up for testing it on camera for six weeks, the application is in the description. You won't have to quit anything. That's the point."

## Story insurance

| If… | Then… |
|---|---|
| The subject withdraws | Wellbeing comes first. Don't use the footage without renewed consent. Keep a second subject in casting for every episode. |
| The decision is "off the table" | That's a complete story. Frame it as time saved, using the site's own line: six weeks, not six years. |
| Nothing dramatic happens | Lean into realism: the arithmetic and the notes *are* the drama for this audience. Don't manufacture conflict. |
| The subject's employer finds out and objects | Stop, protect the subject, and take advice before continuing. |
| The low point is too personal | Leave it out. The episode can carry that in one line of VO: "Week three was hard." |
