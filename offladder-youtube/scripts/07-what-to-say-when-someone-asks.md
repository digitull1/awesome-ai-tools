# Script 07 · What to Say When Someone Asks What You Want to Be

| | |
|---|---|
| **Series** | Honest Answers (teen) |
| **Target length** | 6:30 |
| **Central question** | What do I say, and do, when I have no idea what I want to be? |
| **Viewer** | Students aged 13–18, and the parents they share it with |
| **Register** | Plain and warm. Short sentences. No slang that will date. The adult host talks *with* teenagers, never down to them. |
| **Titles** | A: *What to Say When Someone Asks What You Want to Be* · B: *You're 15 and Have No Idea What You Want to Do. Good.* · C: *No Idea What You Want to Be? Try This This Weekend* |
| **Thumbnail** | A: host mid-laugh, a speech bubble reading *"SO… WHAT DO YOU WANT TO BE?"*, orange box **SAY THIS.** · B: host with a shrug and a smile, orange box **NO IDEA? GOOD.** |
| **Safety** | **No minors on camera.** The skit is shot point-of-view: the camera *is* the teenager, and only an adult actor appears. Every experiment passes the teen-safety rule: 15–30 minutes, no money, no strangers, stop whenever. |

## [0:00–0:20] Cold open: the POV skit

**PICTURE:** Point of view at a family gathering. Plates, noise, a kitchen doorway. An adult relative (adult actor, released) leans into the lens, smiling.

**RELATIVE:** "So! What do you want to be when you leave school?"

**PICTURE:** A slow push in on the relative's expectant face. Room noise fades. A clock ticks.

**ON SCREEN:** THE PAUSE.

**PICTURE:** Hard cut to the host.

> **Retention:** The Pause (hook archetype 1) as a skit. Point of view puts the viewer inside the moment, and it keeps minors off camera entirely.

---

## [0:20–0:50] You're not supposed to know

**HOST:** "That pause. Everyone's had it. Here's the thing: nobody sensible expects a fifteen-year-old to name one job and walk towards it for forty years. You're not supposed to know yet."

**HOST:** "But you can have a better answer than 'um'. Here it is."

**ON SCREEN:** **SAY THIS:** *"I'm testing a couple of directions."*

> **Retention:** The promised line arrives in under a minute. Paying off the title early builds trust, and the rest of the video makes the line true.

---

## [0:50–1:40] Three versions of the line

**HOST:** "There are three versions, depending on the day."

**ON SCREEN:** **THE HONEST ONE:** *"I don't know yet. On purpose. I'm trying things first."*

**ON SCREEN:** **THE FUNNY ONE:** *"I'm running experiments. Results pending."*

**ON SCREEN:** **THE SPECIFIC ONE:** *"I tried [something] last weekend. Liked the [part]. Not sure about the [part]."*

**HOST:** "The specific one is the best, because it ends the conversation *and* makes you sound more sorted than most adults. But it only works if you've actually tried something. So let's make it true."

> **Retention:** Three options feel generous, and a funny one earns a laugh. "Let's make it true" pivots the video from a comeback to a plan.

---

## [1:40–2:30] Why trying beats deciding

**HOST:** "Most careers advice asks you to pick a job title. But job titles hide what the work is actually like. 'Vet' sounds like animals. It's also paperwork, hard conversations with owners, and nights on call. The only way to know if you'd like the *hours* is to try a tiny piece of them."

**ON SCREEN:** EXPLORE → TRY → LEARN → UPDATE

**HOST:** "And there's real work out there right now that the adults asking you have probably never heard of."

**ON SCREEN:** Two Evidence Cards, linking to offladder.com/directions:
- **DIGITAL ACCESSIBILITY ENGINEER:** making websites work for people who use a keyboard, voice or a screen reader.
- **DRONE SURVEY OPERATOR:** flying drones to check crops, roofs and flood risk, then turning what they see into decisions.

**[FACT-CHECK: descriptions match offladder.com/directions at the time of publishing]**

**HOST:** "Quick thing: every week on this channel we test a real job, a small piece of it, on camera. If that sounds fun, subscribe."

> **Retention:** Surprising job names are a micro-payoff for this audience. The subscribe line lands right after it.

## [2:30–4:45] Four tiny experiments for this weekend

**HOST:** "Here are four experiments. Each one takes fifteen to thirty minutes. No money. No strangers. Stop whenever you like."

**ON SCREEN:** 15–30 MIN · NO MONEY · NO STRANGERS · STOP WHENEVER

**ON SCREEN:** 1 · CURIOUS ABOUT DESIGN?

**HOST:** "Find one sign, form or menu that confuses people. Redesign it on paper. Then explain your three changes to someone at home."

**PICTURE:** Hands redrawing a confusing sign. Before and after.

**ON SCREEN:** 2 · CURIOUS ABOUT PSYCHOLOGY?

**HOST:** "Run one tiny, harmless experiment on three friends, and ask them first. For example: show two logos, wait a minute, and see which one they remember. Write down what surprised you."

**ON SCREEN:** 3 · CURIOUS ABOUT JOURNALISM?

**HOST:** "Write two hundred words about something happening on your street, using only what you can see and what people you already know can tell you."

**ON SCREEN:** 4 · CURIOUS ABOUT ENGINEERING?

**HOST:** "Take apart something that's *already broken*, unplugged, with no batteries in it, and with an adult's OK. Then explain how it was supposed to work."

**HOST:** "And if you want to talk to someone who actually does a job you're curious about, don't message strangers online. Ask an adult you trust to introduce you."

> **Retention:** Four fast, visual beats of about 30 seconds each. The safety conditions are part of the content, not small print, which also reassures parents watching.

## [4:45–5:30] Keep score in three lines

**HOST:** "Afterwards, write three lines. Which part went fastest? What did you put off? Would you do it again next week without anyone asking you?"

**ON SCREEN:** The Scorecard graphic, with the three lines ticking.

**HOST:** "If you hated it? Brilliant. That's a real result. You found out in half an hour instead of in a two-year course. Write down which part you hated. That detail usually points at something next door you might love."
