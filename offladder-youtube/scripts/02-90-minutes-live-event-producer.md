# Script 02 · I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Series** | 90 Minutes As… (pilot episode) |
| **Target length** | 14:00 |
| **Central question** | Would I actually want to do this job twice? |
| **Viewer** | Students, and anyone of any age who organises things for fun |
| **Direction** | Live event production: *"Coordinate people, timings and suppliers when plans keep moving."* (OffLadder's own description) |
| **Titles** *(outcome-dependent)* | A: *I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes (Then the Supplier Cancelled)* · B: *I Had 90 Minutes to Plan a Festival. Then It Fell Apart.* (only if it genuinely did) · C: *Can a Complete Beginner Plan a Festival in 90 Minutes?* |
| **Thumbnail** | Archetype A: host on the phone, hand on forehead, the run-of-show taped to the wall behind. Badge: the real time on the clock when the call came (for example **43:10**). Text: **NO STAGE.** |
| **Structure** | The Experiment Arc ([04-story-and-retention.md](../04-story-and-retention.md#structure-1-the-experiment-arc)) |

> **Precondition:** this pilot works only if *live event production* genuinely appears in the host's own OffLadder result. Run the three questions in pre-production and screen-record it. If live events doesn't come up, pilot with a direction that does. Never fake the result.

---

## Pre-production

### People
- **Host:** a genuine beginner at event production.
- **Practitioner:** a working live event producer. Pay them for their time, and have them co-design the brief and the curveball so both are realistic. **[FACT-CHECK: their credits, as described on screen]**
- **Crew:** two cameras (a wide, and a host close-up), a top-down action camera over the table, lavalier mics on both people, and a separate recorder on the practitioner's phone for the curveball call.

### Location
A room with a big blank wall (the run-of-show is built on it with sticky notes and tape) and a table. The practitioner's real office is ideal: it adds truth to every cutaway.

### The honesty rules for this episode
1. **The festival is a practice brief**, designed by the practitioner and based on real events they've run. Say so on screen.
2. **The curveball is planned.** The practitioner chooses what and when; the host doesn't know either. Say so on screen when it happens.
3. **The clock is real.** A physical timer runs on set, and the on-screen 90:00 countdown is synced to it. We cut for length, but the countdown always shows the true time remaining.
4. **The Scorecard and the verdict are the host's real answers**, recorded within 30 minutes of finishing.

### The brief pack
Build this with the practitioner. Every number in it is part of a fictional scenario and must be labelled that way on screen.

| Item | Contents |
|---|---|
| The event | A free, one-day community festival in a park, 11:00–19:00 |
| Scale | Around 500 people across the day *(scenario figure)* |
| On site | One small stage with sound, twelve food stalls, a craft tent for children, a first-aid point, hired toilets, fifteen volunteers |
| Venue map | The park, with one vehicle gate, a bandstand and an access road |
| Constraints | All vehicles off site by 10:30; a 19:00 noise curfew *(scenario)* |
| Budget sheet | A simple, fictional sheet with a small contingency line |
| Deliverables | 1. A run-of-show (the minute-by-minute plan for the day) · 2. A load-in schedule (who arrives when, through which gate) · 3. A one-page contingency plan |
| The curveball (sealed, practitioner only) | At about 45 minutes elapsed, the practitioner phones the host as the stage and sound supplier: their truck has broken down and they can't come. It's 08:00 on the day and doors open at 11:00. |
| The pro's pocket list (practitioner only) | What a professional would already have ready: backup supplier contacts, the bandstand as a fallback stage, acoustic-friendly acts, a public announcement template. Revealed during the review. |

### Practitioner pre-interview (capture before the experiment)
1. "What does a live event producer actually do, in one sentence?"
2. "What did last Tuesday look like, hour by hour?"
3. "Which part of the job would you delete if you could?"
4. "What does the worst week of your year look like?"
5. "Tell me about a real time a supplier let you down on the day."
6. "How do people usually get into this? What do they grow into it from?"
7. "What's the smallest real version of this job someone could try this week?"

## [0:00–0:25] Beat 1 · Cold open: the call

**PICTURE:** Handheld on the host at a table buried in printouts. The phone rings, and the host answers on speaker.

**[CAPTURE: the host's real reaction to the call. Two angles, plus the practitioner's side of the audio.]**

*Expected shape (don't prompt it):* "…Sorry, the whole stage? And the sound?" A beat. "Right. Okay. Okay."

**ON SCREEN:** The clock, frozen at the real time remaining (for example **43:10**).

**PICTURE:** The host lowers the phone and looks at the wall. Series sting: one second, orange **90:00** block.

**ON SCREEN:** **{real elapsed} MINUTES EARLIER** *(clock rewinds to 90:00)*

> **Retention:** In medias res (hook archetype 4). The viewer has now seen the disaster the title promised, which confirms the click, and needs the backstory. The frozen clock plants a question: how much time is left to fix it?

---

## [0:25–1:25] Beat 2 · Why this job

**PICTURE:** The host at home on the sofa, screen-recording the phone while answering OffLadder's three questions. Filmed during pre-production.

**VO (draft; rewrite to match the real result):** "I didn't pick today's job. I answered three questions on OffLadder, the app we make, and it gave me two directions to test. One of them was live event production: coordinating people, timings and suppliers when plans keep moving." **[HOST: real. Show the actual two directions on screen.]**

**HOST:** *(to camera, real reason)* "Which, honestly, sounds like my idea of a nightmare. So obviously that's the one we're testing." **[HOST: real. Use your true reason for choosing it.]**

**PICTURE:** Meet the practitioner at their office. A lower third with name and role.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** their one-sentence description of the job, from pre-interview question 1.

> **Retention:** The product works as a plot device. It drives the story instead of interrupting it. The host's honest reluctance adds a stake: will the "nightmare" turn out to fit?

---

## [1:25–2:00] Beat 3 · The rules

**HOST:** *(to camera, the wall behind)* "Here's the deal. Ninety minutes. [Name] gives me a real brief, a one-day festival. I build the plan the way they would. At some point, something's going to go wrong, because that's the job. At the end, three questions: which twenty minutes went fastest, what did I put off, and would I do it again next week without being asked? Then a verdict. More, variant, or off the table."

**ON SCREEN:** The Scorecard graphic, and the three verdict stamps in a row.

**HOST:** "Before we start, what's your guess? Comment more, variant or off the table. I'll pin the best reasoning."

**ON SCREEN:** **YOUR GUESS? MORE · VARIANT · OFF THE TABLE**

**PICTURE:** The practitioner starts the physical timer. The on-screen clock starts at 90:00.

> **Retention:** The Verdict Loop opens, with the payoff promised for the end. The prediction prompt turns watching into a bet; viewers stay to find out whether they were right.

---

## [2:00–4:15] Beat 4 · First contact

**PICTURE:** The host reads the brief pack. Close-ups of the venue map, the budget sheet, the deliverables list.

**ON SCREEN:** Small caption: **PRACTICE BRIEF DESIGNED BY [NAME], BASED ON EVENTS [NAME] HAS RUN**

**[CAPTURE: the first moment of overwhelm, the host's first question to the practitioner, and the first sticky note going on the wall.]**

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** explaining what a run-of-show is, in their own words.

**VO (draft):** "I thought event production was clipboards and headsets. For the first twenty minutes it was {the real texture: spreadsheets, maps, phone calls}." **[HOST: real]**

**ON SCREEN:** Split screen: **IMAGINED** (a headset, a crowd) against **ACTUAL** (the host's real screen or the wall).

**PICTURE:** A time-lapse of the wall filling up. The clock ticks down in the corner.

> **Retention:** Expectation against reality is a micro-payoff. The time-lapse compresses the dull part while the clock keeps the stakes visible.

---

## [4:15–5:20] Beat 5 · First check-in

**PICTURE:** The practitioner walks up to the wall and reads it in silence. The host waits.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** the first real critique. *Expected shape:* a clash the host missed, such as food vans and stage crew needing the one gate at the same time. Never scripted; use whatever they genuinely spot.

**HOST (capture):** the reaction.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** one professional tip.

> **Retention:** Expert judgement creates tension, because a judge's opinion matters. The tip is a small, true payoff about how the job works.

---

## [5:20–7:00] Beat 6 · The curveball

**PICTURE:** The host working. The practitioner steps out of the room.

**ON SCREEN:** Small caption: **THE NEXT CALL WAS PLANNED BY [NAME]. [HOST] DIDN'T KNOW WHAT, OR WHEN.**

**PICTURE:** The phone rings. We play the whole call this time, including what the cold open cut. The clock catches up to the frozen time from the opening.

**[CAPTURE: the host's first idea after hanging up, said out loud. Keep the false starts.]**

**VO (draft):** "It's eight in the morning on the day. Doors open at eleven. There's no stage, there's no sound, and there are {real minutes} left on the clock."

**ON SCREEN:** **08:00 ON THE DAY · DOORS 11:00 · NO STAGE · NO SOUND**

> **Retention:** The midpoint reset. The story reaches the cold open, and a brand-new question opens: can this be saved? The caption keeps us honest without draining the tension, because the host's reaction is still real.

---

## [7:00–9:30] Beat 7 · Recovery

**PICTURE:** The re-plan: crossing things out, phone calls to the practitioner (who stays in role as "the supplier" or "the council" as agreed), the map.

**[CAPTURE: at least one idea that fails, and the moment something clicks, such as spotting the bandstand on the map.]**

**VO (draft):** "The first plan was {real}. It lasted about {real} minutes." **[HOST: real]**

**ON SCREEN:** The clock under two minutes, then under one. A tick sound. **00:00** in orange.

**HOST (capture):** a real line at time-up.

> **Retention:** The Stakes Ladder: a private plan becomes a rescue attempt. The clock hitting zero is a built-in climax.

---

## [9:30–11:00] Beat 8 · The review

**PICTURE:** The practitioner reads the final plan. Silence. Hold on it.

**HOST:** "Okay. What would you fix?"

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** an honest critique. Ask for specifics: *"What would have happened at 10:40?"*

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** the reveal of the pro's pocket list: *"Here's what I'd have had ready before the day even started."*

**HOST:** "Honestly, could I do this job?"

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** a straight answer. Keep it, however it lands.

> **Retention:** A judge's verdict before the host's own verdict. "Could I do this job?" is the most rewatched kind of moment in challenge formats, so give it room and don't cut away early.

---

## [11:00–11:45] Beat 9 · The real job

**PICTURE:** Pre-interview footage, in the practitioner's office.

**PRACTITIONER (capture):** the part they'd delete (question 3), the worst week (question 4), and how people get in (question 6).

**ON SCREEN:** An Evidence Card for pay, **only** from a primary source with its date (for example the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics *Occupational Outlook Handbook* entry for meeting, convention and event planners, or the official careers service in the viewer's country). **[FACT-CHECK: current figure, date, source]**

> **Retention:** The held-back reveal. "The part they'd delete" is often the most honest line in the episode, and it sets up the viewer's own judgement.

---

## [11:45–12:55] Beat 10 · Scorecard and verdict

**PICTURE:** Diary camera: the host alone, somewhere quiet, recorded within 30 minutes of finishing.

**HOST:** "Three questions. Which twenty minutes went fastest?" **[HOST: real]**

**ON SCREEN:** The first question ticks.

**HOST:** "What did I put off?" **[HOST: real]**

**HOST:** "Would I do it again next week, without anyone asking?" **[HOST: real]**

**PICTURE:** The host writes the verdict and one written reason on a card, face down.

**SOUND:** Silence. No music for two full seconds.

**PICTURE:** The card turns over. An orange stamp: **MORE**, **VARIANT** or **OFF THE TABLE**.

**HOST:** reads the written reason. *(A reason is compulsory. For example: "Variant. I loved untangling the schedule. I'd avoid anything budget-heavy." Use the real one.)* **[HOST: real]**

> **Retention:** The loop opened at 1:25 closes. The silence before the stamp is the most important sound decision in the episode. A reason makes the verdict useful instead of theatrical.

---

## [12:55–13:45] Beat 11 · Your Version

**HOST:** "You don't need a festival to try this. Here's your version. This week, plan a small, real thing for real people: a birthday dinner, a study session, a five-a-side game. Then, on purpose, cancel one piece of it on yourself. The venue, the ball, the main course. Give yourself ten minutes to re-plan."

**ON SCREEN:** A Your Version card: **1. PLAN A SMALL REAL THING · 2. CANCEL ONE PIECE ON PURPOSE · 3. RE-PLAN IN 10 MINUTES**

**HOST:** "Afterwards, one question: did the untangling feel like a puzzle, or like a headache? That answer is worth more than any quiz."

**HOST:** "You don't have to test live events, either. If you want your own two directions and a first experiment, it's three questions at offladder.com. Free, no account."

**ON SCREEN:** Lower third: **OFFLADDER.COM · 3 QUESTIONS · FREE**

> **Retention:** Value first. The Your Version echoes the episode's own twist, so it feels earned, and the product line arrives after the payoff.

---

**HOST:** "If you enjoyed watching me panic about a festival, the next one's worse. I had ninety minutes to make an AI fail on purpose. It's a real job, and it's right here."
