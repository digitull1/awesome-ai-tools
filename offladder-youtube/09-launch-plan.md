# 09 · Launch plan: the first 90 days

> Launch with a small library, not a single video, so the first viewers have somewhere to go next. Then publish on a steady rhythm and review on a fixed schedule.

---

## Overview

| Phase | Weeks | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Research and set-up** | −4 to −1 | Validate the language, build the channel, bank three long-form videos and ten Shorts, and start filming Change One Thing |
| **Launch** | 1 | Publish two long-form videos together, so the first viewers can binge |
| **Rhythm** | 2–12 | One long-form video and three Shorts a week, with checkpoints at weeks 4, 8 and 12 |

The first quarter leans on **Honest Answers**. They're the cheapest to make and they build search traffic while the heavier series are in production.

---

## Pre-launch

### Week -4: research sprint

Five days that stop us building on assumptions.

1. **Search language.** Type 30 seed phrases into YouTube search ("I don't know what career", "career change at", "jobs AI", "what should I study", "day in the life of a") and record the autocomplete suggestions. Adjust the titles of videos 01, 03, 04, 07, 11 and 15 to match how people actually phrase the questions.
2. **The competitive feed.** Collect 30 videos a target viewer is likely to see next to ours: career change, day-in-the-life, AI and jobs, study choices. Record each one's title formula, thumbnail archetype and length. This becomes the mock feed for thumbnail testing ([02-packaging.md](02-packaging.md#46-thumbnail-qa-before-upload)).
3. **Gap check.** Test the strategy's category read ([01-strategy.md](01-strategy.md#2-why-this-can-win)) against what the 30 videos actually do. Change the plan if we're wrong.
4. **Hook test.** Read the hooks for videos 01, 02 and 03 aloud to five people from the target audiences. Note where attention visibly drops.
5. **Lock the first six.** Confirm the launch slate, and book practitioners for videos 02, 05 and 08.

## Channel copy

**About section**
```text
Don't pick your future. Test it.

Every week we try a real piece of a job, ask the people who do it which part they'd delete, and tell you honestly how it felt. Every video ends with a small experiment you can run yourself.

Made by the team behind OffLadder, the career app that learns from what you actually try. Three questions, free, no account: offladder.com

New videos every [day].
```

| 0–4 s | POV skit: a relative leans into the lens, *"So! What do you want to be?"* | Room noise, then a clock tick |

## Checkpoints

### Checkpoint 1 · end of week 4
*Question: is the packaging working?*
- Which thumbnail archetypes won Test & Compare? Write the first house rule.
- Which hook archetype has the best 0:30 retention so far?
- Are Shorts feeding long-form views through related-video links?
- Is UTM tracking recording three-question starts correctly?

### Checkpoint 2 · end of week 8
*Question: which series earn their slots?*
- Place every long-form video in the diagnosis matrix ([08-analytics.md](08-analytics.md#3-the-diagnosis-matrix)).
- Rank the series on CTR, average percentage viewed, subscribers per 1,000 views and three-question starts.
- Reallocate weeks 9–12 towards the best performers where production allows.
- Decide whether to cast a second Change One Thing.

### Checkpoint 3 · end of week 12
*Question: what does the next quarter look like?*
- A full retrospective with post-mortems for all 13 long-form videos.
- Rewrite the parts of this playbook our data contradicts.
- Set the next quarter's slate and cadence, and decide whether to add a second weekly long-form slot.
- Report to OffLadder leadership: YouTube-tagged three-question starts by series, the cost per video, and what we'd change.

## If one person is doing all of this

A lean version that still compounds:

- **One long-form video every two weeks**, alternating Honest Answers and 90 Minutes As…
- **Three Shorts a week**, batch-shot in one half-day every fortnight.
- **Skip Change One Thing** until there's a production coordinator. It's the series that needs the most care.
- **Keep every gate.** CLICK, the claims register and the publish checklist are what make a small channel look big.
