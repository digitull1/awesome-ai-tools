# 01 · Channel strategy

> **The thesis in one line:** OffLadder's method (try a small, real piece of a career before you choose one) is already one of YouTube's most dependable formats: the on-camera experiment. The channel films the method.

> **Update, 28 September 2026: lead with AI anxiety.** The fear that AI will change or remove the work people are doing or studying for is shared across every age and career stage (52% of US workers say they're worried, [Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/02/25/u-s-workers-are-more-worried-than-hopeful-about-future-ai-use-in-the-workplace/)). It's also the problem OffLadder was built for. So the Shorts now lead with **The ladder is breaking** ([scripts/ai-series.md](scripts/ai-series.md)): verbatim quotes from the people building AI and sourced figures, then the approach (*nobody knows which jobs will last, so test before you bet*), then OffLadder's adaptive loop as the answer. The AI-Anxious move from reach engine to the lead Shorts audience; the guardrails in §6 apply unchanged.

> **Update, 28 September 2026 (later): from Short 6, make it personal.** General AI anxiety gets nods, not shares. From episode 6 the Shorts become **Cooked or not?** ([scripts/cooked-or-not.md](scripts/cooked-or-not.md)): each one puts a single job or degree on trial with public data (Microsoft Research's ranking of 785 jobs by AI overlap, NY Fed graduate outcomes, BLS projections), swings a verdict needle with every piece of evidence, and ends *"Comment your job. We'll look it up."* Every comment gets a sourced reply, and the most requested job becomes the next episode. The verdict is never *cooked*: the needle can visit it, but the answer always says what is actually changing and what to test.

## 1. What we are building

A YouTube channel where every video is one of three things:

1. **An experiment.** Someone does the smallest real version of a job on camera and tells the truth about how it felt.
2. **Evidence.** Someone who does the work shows what the hours actually contain, including the part they would delete.
3. **An honest answer.** A direct, useful answer to a question people type into YouTube late at night: *"I don't know what career I want."*

Every video ends the same way, with **one small thing the viewer can try this week**. That ending is the bridge to the product. OffLadder is simply the fastest way to get your next experiment picked for you.

**Channel promise** (banner, trailer, About section):
*Every week we test a real piece of a job, so you can find out what fits without betting years on a guess.*

**Short form:** *We don't guess careers. We test them.*

## 2. Why this can win

This is a qualitative read of the category. Validate it in the research sprint in [09-launch-plan.md](09-launch-plan.md#week--4-research-sprint) before committing the first six videos.

Career content on YouTube clusters into four crowded shapes. Each one leaves an opening that OffLadder is unusually well placed to fill:

| What's crowded | What it gets wrong | OffLadder's opening |
|---|---|---|
| Salary lists ("highest-paying jobs without a degree") | Says what a job pays, not what the hours feel like | Film the hour of work |
| AI-fear lists ("jobs AI will replace", "AI-proof jobs") | Predictions presented as facts, which often disagree with each other (script 03 tests this on camera) | Name the guess, then give viewers a habit that survives any prediction |
| Glossy day-in-the-life vlogs | Shows the best day | Shows the worst week and the part they'd delete |
| Talking-head career advice | Advice with nothing to do afterwards | An experiment the viewer can start before the video ends |

Three structural advantages make this more than a positioning statement:

1. **The format is native.** "I tried X" works on YouTube because it carries built-in stakes (will it go well?) and a built-in payoff (the verdict). OffLadder's method *is* that format, so the product appears in every episode without an ad break. Few career brands can film their product this naturally.
2. **Honesty compounds.** YouTube says its recommendations weigh *valued watchtime*, which it measures with viewer satisfaction surveys, and not clicks alone ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)). Packaging that over-promises wins the click and loses the survey. A channel whose packaging is always true of the footage builds on the exact signal the system is trying to measure. YouTube's own title and thumbnail A/B test also picks winners by watch time, not click-through rate ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)), so misleading packaging loses the test too.
3. **Supply is effectively unlimited and cheap.** Every job contains dozens of activities, and every hobby hides verbs that lead to work nobody has heard of. Most episodes need one practitioner, one location and one afternoon.

## 3. Who we are for

OffLadder serves anyone unsure what's next: people choosing subjects, starting out, changing direction or returning to work. On YouTube we lead with the two audiences whose questions best fit the experiment format. AI anxiety works as the reach engine because it attracts both.

### Priority 1: The Quietly Stuck (27–45)

- **Situation:** Good at their job and, in OffLadder's words, *"quietly certain they do not want twenty more years of it."* Often has a mortgage, maybe children, and no appetite for starting from zero.
- **What they type:** "career change at 40", "I hate my job but don't know what else to do", "what career is right for me", "how to change careers with no experience".
- **What they fear:** wasted years, a pay cut, looking foolish in front of people who depend on them.
- **Why they click:** someone like them, with the same constraints, testing something *without quitting*.
- **Why they stay:** stakes that feel like their own, the money arithmetic, and a real decision at the end.

### Priority 1: The Undecided Student (15–22)

- **Situation:** Choosing subjects, a degree or a first job while everyone asks what they want to be.
- **What they type and watch:** "what should I study", "jobs for people who like [X]", "day in the life of a [job]", and a lot of Shorts.
- **What they fear:** the pause after they say "I don't know", and falling behind friends who seem certain.
- **Why they click:** permission not to know, strange but real jobs, and a person actually trying something.
- **Why they stay:** fast pace, honest failures, humour, and experiments they can do this weekend without asking anyone.

### Lead for Shorts: The AI-Anxious (any age, any stage)

- **Who:** the student choosing what to study, the graduate looking at entry-level roles, the mid-career worker watching their tasks change, the parent. The anxiety cuts across every other audience here.
- **What they type:** "will AI take my job", "AI-proof jobs", "jobs of the future", "what should I study".
- **Why they click:** the people building AI saying it out loud, in their own words, and a number that makes it concrete.
- **Why they stay:** the honest turn (*nobody knows which jobs will last, not even them*), then a move they can make this week, and new kinds of work they'd never have thought of.

### Secondary: The Returner

Returning after caring, illness, redundancy or a long break. Their question is *"where does my experience fit now?"* Serve them through **Change One Thing** episodes and one Honest Answer per quarter.

### Secondary, high-trust conversion: The Parent

A parent of a 13–18-year-old wants to help without pushing. Parents watch less but share more: one parent sends the link to one teenager. Serve them with a small number of dedicated videos, and link every teen-facing video to OffLadder's one-minute page for parents (`offladder.com/for-parents`).

## 4. The format portfolio

Five long-form series and a family of Shorts. Each has a specific job in the portfolio, and none is there for variety alone.

| Series | What it is | Length | Main traffic engine | Job in the portfolio | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **90 Minutes As…** | Our host does the smallest real version of a job, coached by a practitioner, against a 90-minute clock | 12–16 min | Browse (Home) | Flagship: reach and subscribers | Medium |
| **Last Tuesday** | A practitioner rebuilds last Tuesday hour by hour, then names the part they'd delete and the worst week of their year | 9–12 min | Suggested (next to day-in-the-life videos) | Depth and honesty; strong with students | Low–medium |
| **The Job Nobody Told You About** | A mini-documentary on one emerging direction, told OffLadder's way: what the days contain, why it's appearing now, what's genuinely unknown, and one test for this week | 10–13 min | Browse and Suggested | Novelty, curiosity and AI topicality | Medium–high |
| **Change One Thing** | A real person tests a career change over six weeks without quitting, and makes a decision on camera | 18–25 min | Suggested (next to career-change and "I quit" videos) | Emotional retention and trust; the proof that the method works on real lives | High |
| **Honest Answers** | A direct answer to a high-intent search question, built around exercises the viewer does while watching | 7–11 min | Search | Evergreen search traffic and the highest conversion to OffLadder | Low |
| **Shorts: The ladder is breaking** (lead), plus Delete One Part, Hobby → Verb, The Pause, Real or Not? | Vertical, 20–60 seconds (Shorts can now run to 3 minutes, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)) | ≤ 60 s | Shorts feed | Discovery, teenage reach and comment loops | Very low |

**Default cadence:** one long-form video per week on a four-week rotation (Honest Answers → 90 Minutes As… → The Job Nobody Told You About → Last Tuesday), plus one **Change One Thing** roughly every six to eight weeks. Add three to five Shorts a week. The 12-week calendar is in [09-launch-plan.md](09-launch-plan.md).

## 5. Signature devices

These are the recurring elements that make an OffLadder video recognisable within ten seconds, even with the sound off. Each one comes from OffLadder's own method, which is what keeps the channel and the product one idea.

| Device | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **The Scorecard** | Three questions asked after every experiment: *Which twenty minutes went fastest? What did I put off? Would I do it again next week without being asked?* | OffLadder's "how to keep score" method |
| **The Verdict** | Every experiment ends in one of three stamps: **MORE**, **VARIANT** or **OFF THE TABLE**, always with a written reason | The six-week decision in OffLadder's career-change method |
| **Your Version** | The final segment. A viewer-sized experiment of 15–90 minutes that needs no money, no permission and no strangers | "One small thing to try" on every OffLadder page |
| **The 90:00 Clock** | An on-screen countdown in experiment episodes | *"The gap between 'I wonder about that' and 'I know something about that' is usually about ninety minutes."* |
| **The Delete Question** | Every practitioner is asked: *"Which part of your job would you delete if you could?"* | OffLadder's advice on informational interviews |
| **Evidence Cards** | On-screen source cards (organisation, report and year) whenever a fact appears | OffLadder's editorial rule: *"We only state what can be checked."* |

The Verdict stamp, the Clock and the orange highlight box also carry through into thumbnails. See [02-packaging.md](02-packaging.md#4-thumbnail-system).

## 6. Guardrails (non-negotiable)

These come straight from how offladder.com writes. They are also good strategy: in a category built on exaggeration, trust is the differentiator.

1. **No invented statistics.** Every number on screen or in voiceover needs a primary source, shown on an Evidence Card. If we can't source it, we cut it, or we generate our own evidence on camera and say how small the sample is ("we asked 40 people on one street, which proves nothing, but look at this").
2. **No "AI-proof jobs" lists, no "one perfect career", no predictions presented as facts.** We can, and should, make videos that *challenge* those lists.
3. **No salary numbers in titles or thumbnails.** Pay can appear inside a video only as a sourced, dated range on an Evidence Card.
4. **Packaging must be true of the footage.** Draft titles and thumbnails before the shoot, then finalise them *after the edit*. If the footage doesn't support the title, change the title, not the footage. Every script in this repo gives alternative titles for different outcomes.
5. **Describe OffLadder honestly.** It suggests directions to test. It does not choose careers or find "your perfect job". Laddie is an AI guide and can be wrong, so we say so: *"check anything important."*
6. **Assume a 13-year-old is watching.** Any experiment we recommend to viewers must be safe for them: 15–30 minutes, no money, no contacting strangers without a trusted adult, stop whenever. This mirrors OffLadder's under-16 guidance.
7. **Minors on camera: avoid by default.** If a teenager appears, get written parental consent, show no school names or locations, and moderate comments actively. YouTube may restrict features, including comments, on videos featuring minors ([YouTube child safety policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999)).
8. **Keep OffLadder's privacy promise.** Never mine users' answers or Laddie conversations for content ideas, stories or case studies. OffLadder tells users their answers are used to help them explore *"and nothing else."* Case studies come only from people who apply to be filmed and sign a release.
9. **Label synthetic media.** When we show realistic AI-generated or altered media (for example, the fake photos in the provenance episode), we label it on screen and use YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)).
10. **Name the fear, never amplify it.** We can say "a lot of people are scared AI will take their job." We never manufacture urgency ("do this before it's too late").

> **Honesty is not a click-through tax.** Our click-through engine is *curiosity*, not exaggeration. A curiosity gap is honest when the video closes it. Every title in this system passes one test: *would the viewer feel the promise was kept?*

## 7. How YouTube feeds the product

```
Watch a video
  → do "Your Version" (the experiment, while the motivation is fresh)
  → offladder.com/start: 3 questions, free, no account, about 2 minutes
  → two directions worth testing + one thing to try this week
  → save them with a free account → tell Laddie how the experiment went
  → weekly brief → the constellation grows → (optional) membership
```

- **YouTube's job** is to create the *"I could try that"* moment and to show that the method works. It is not the place to sell membership. We never mention prices on camera, because founding pricing changes, and "free to start" is the honest, lower-friction message.
- **Series and CTA strength vary.** Honest Answers viewers arrive with a question the product answers, so they get the clearest product call to action. Experiment viewers arrive for the story, so the product appears *inside* the story (the host's own three-question result picks the episode's direction). See [05-ctas.md](05-ctas.md).
- **Everything is tracked.** Every link carries UTM parameters so the site can count three-question starts by series and by video. See [05-ctas.md](05-ctas.md#9-tracking-links).

## 8. What success looks like

**North-star metric:** monthly three-question starts on offladder.com from YouTube (UTM-tracked).

**Channel health metrics** (reviewed with the rhythm in [08-analytics.md](08-analytics.md)):

| Metric | Why it matters | Compare against |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR, by traffic source | Is the packaging working for cold audiences? | Our own rolling median |
| Share still watching at 0:30 ("Intro" in Studio) | Did the opening confirm the click? | Our own rolling median, by series |
| Average percentage viewed | Did the story hold? | Same series, similar length |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | Did the channel promise land? | Our own rolling median |
| Returning viewers | Are we becoming a habit? | Month on month |
| End-screen click rate | Do viewers want the next one? | Our own rolling median |
| UTM sessions → 3-question starts | Does the channel feed the product? | By series |

We deliberately benchmark against **ourselves**, not internet averages. The one external reference worth knowing: YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, with a wider range for new videos and lower CTR when impressions come from the Home page ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)).

**Set expectations honestly.** Nobody can promise a video will spread, and we won't pretend otherwise. This system raises the hit rate and makes every video a controlled test we learn from. Plan on judging the channel after roughly 12 long-form videos, not after three.

## 9. What we will not make

- Reaction videos, drama, or commentary on other creators.
- Fake shock faces in thumbnails. We use real moments captured on set.
- "Hey guys, welcome back" openings, long logo stings, or subscribe requests in the first minute.
- Rankings of "best" or "worst" careers. They contradict the whole thesis that fit comes from testing.
- Anything a 13-year-old shouldn't copy.
