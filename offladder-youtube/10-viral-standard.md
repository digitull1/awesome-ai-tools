# 10 · The viral standard for TikTok, Reels and Shorts

*Added 28 September 2026. From now on, every short-form video OffLadder makes is built to this standard, once, as a single master that works on all three platforms.*

The goal is short-form video people watch to the end, watch again, and **send to someone**, because it's beautiful, it asks something of them, and it's true. Everything below comes from what the platforms say they reward, what research says makes people share, and the rules OffLadder already keeps.

## 2. Why people share

- **Arousal, not just feeling.** Across every *New York Times* article over three months, content that evoked high-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety) was shared more, and low-arousal emotion (sadness) less. Positive content beat negative overall ([Berger and Milkman, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353)). So we pair the anxiety people already feel about AI with **awe** (data made beautiful) and **relief** (a move they can make), never with gloom.
- **STEPPS** ([Berger, *Contagious*](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/contagious-jonah-berger-on-why-things-catch-on/)): people share what makes them look good (**social currency**: "did you know Microsoft ranked 785 jobs?"), what's top of mind (**triggers**: results day, graduation, layoffs news), what moves them (**emotion**), what's visible (**public**: comment your number), what's useful (**practical value**: a test for tonight) and what's a story (**stories**).
- **Curiosity is a gap.** Curiosity rises when attention falls on a gap between what we know and what we want to know ([Loewenstein, *Psychological Bulletin*, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75)). Open the gap in second one; close it after second 15.

## 3. The ten laws

1. **The first frame is the hook and the cover.** A question or claim in seven words or fewer, plus a picture that makes sense on mute. It must stop a thumb at thumbnail size.
2. **Motion within 0.3 seconds, first cut by 1.5.** Nothing opens on a still.
3. **Open a gap in second one. Close it late.** Countdowns, verdicts, reveals, "#1 will surprise you" only if it will.
4. **Give the viewer a job.** Pick one, guess, rank, spot the odd one out. Interaction turns a viewer into a participant, and participants comment.
5. **A new beat every two seconds.** A cut, a reveal, a camera move, a sound. The eye never gets bored.
6. **Visual first.** Data art, icons, boards, meters and motion carry the story; text is the caption. No frame is mostly text.
7. **Sound on, sound designed.** An original score in the OffLadder style, every hit landing on its frame, -14 LUFS, and tested through a phone-speaker filter.
8. **Build for the send.** Every video names who it's for ("send this to someone who…"), and gives the viewer an identity to recognise ("I'm a 3").
9. **End on the loop.** The last frame rebuilds the first, so a rewatch starts without a seam.
10. **True, and ends with agency.** Every figure is sourced on screen. The turn is always towards something to try, and OffLadder is how.

## 4. The formats

Each is built once, as one master, for TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

| Format | The job it gives the viewer | Why it spreads | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pick one** | Choose one of four things you'd happily do on a Sunday | Identity ("I'm a 3"), comments, sends to friends ("you're a 2"), rewatches to see every answer. It *is* OffLadder's promise: work you'd be good at but never thought of | *Pick one. It shows jobs you'd never have thought of* |
| **Tier list** | Argue with the ranking | Tier lists are native to TikTok and built for debate; the data makes it credible | *15 jobs, tiered by Microsoft's AI data. Where's yours?* |

## 5. Hooks

Every hook is true, fits in seven words, and works with the sound off.

- **Choice:** *Pick one.* · *Which would you do for free?* · *1, 2, 3 or 4? Choose fast.*
- **Rank:** *Microsoft ranked 785 jobs. Guess #1.* · *15 jobs, tiered by AI.* · *Is yours in the top 10?*
- **Verdict:** *Is a CS degree cooked?* · *Are writers cooked?* · *Cooked or not: nursing.*
- **Contradiction:** *CS grads: 7% unemployed. Art history: 6.7%.* · *The #1 job on the list is still growing.*
- **Receipts:** *"Learn to code" is dead? Check the data.* · *Your careers adviser was wrong about this.*
- **Identity:** *If you're the friend who fixes everything…* · *For everyone who hates their degree.*
- **POV:** *POV: telling your mum what you want to study.* · *POV: your careers adviser finds out about AI.*
- **Stakes:** *2.9 million US workers do this job.* · *The degree everyone called safe.*
- **Quote:** *"Probably none of us will have a job."* (verbatim, with who and when on screen)

## 6. Craft

**Picture.** 1080×1920, 30 fps, motion blur on fast moves, full-bleed (never borders). OffLadder's palette (ink, paper, orange) plus the heat ramp for data. Archivo Black for claims, Instrument Serif italic for the human voice, Hind for the small print. One idea per frame; the eye always knows where to look. Big graphic objects (boards, maps, meters, cards, icons) carry the story.

**Sound.** Two house styles from the score synthesiser: *epic* (strings, choir, taiko, braams) for reveals and verdicts, and *beat* (punchy drums, sub bass, plucks, claps) for games and lists. Every entrance has a sound; every reveal lands on a downbeat. A short OffLadder motif closes every video, so the sound itself becomes recognisable. Masters at -14 LUFS, checked through a phone-speaker filter.

**The safe zone.** Anything that must be read sits inside **x 65–900, y 270–1250**. That box clears Instagram's interface (Meta asks for the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side, per its [Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490/)), TikTok's right-hand buttons and caption, and YouTube's. Decoration can bleed to the edges. Instagram's profile grid crops covers to 3:4, so the hook sits in the middle of the frame. The renderer checks this for every piece that opts in (`safe: 'social'`).

## 7. Posting

- **One master, native uploads.** The same file goes to TikTok, Reels and Shorts, uploaded natively (never a download with another platform's watermark), with platform-specific captions.
- **Captions:** the hook line with its keywords first, one question for the comments, three to five hashtags. On TikTok the caption is searchable, so say the job and the topic in words.
- **First comment:** the job the video gives the viewer ("Comment your number"). Pin it.
- **Trial reels** once there's a following to protect: they test a new format on non-followers first, and Instagram can share the winners with followers. While the account is new, post regular Reels so the grid fills with the best work (a visitor decides to follow from the grid).
- **Reply** to every comment in the first two hours; on TikTok, answer the best ones with a reply video.
- **Measure what the platforms measure:** Instagram (watch time, sends per reach, likes per reach), TikTok (average watch time, completion, shares, comments), YouTube (viewed vs swiped away, average percentage viewed). Make a part two of anything that beats the channel's median by half again.

## 8. The proud test

Nothing ships until every answer is yes.

- [ ] Would I stop scrolling for the first frame, on mute, at thumbnail size?
- [ ] Is it clear within one second what I'm being asked to do or find out?
- [ ] Does something change at least every two seconds?
- [ ] Is every frame more picture than text?
- [ ] Does the sound make it better with the volume up, and does it still work on a phone speaker?
- [ ] Is there someone specific a viewer would send this to?
- [ ] Is every number sourced on screen, and would it survive a fact-check in the comments?
- [ ] Does it end with something to try, and a seamless loop?
- [ ] Is all readable text inside the safe zone on all three platforms?
- [ ] Would I put it at the top of my showreel?
