# 04 · Story structures and retention

> Retention isn't editing tricks. It's a question the viewer needs answered, a reason to believe the answer is coming, and nothing in the way.

Every OffLadder video is built on one **central question**, written on the first page of the script:

- *90 Minutes As…:* "Would I actually want to do this job twice?"
- *Last Tuesday:* "What do the hours of this job really contain?"
- *The Job Nobody Told You About:* "What is this work, and could it be for me?"
- *Change One Thing:* "Will this person change their life, and should they?"
- *Honest Answers:* "What do I actually do about [my question]?"

If a scene doesn't move the viewer closer to the answer, or deliberately delay it in a way that raises the stakes, it gets cut.

## Part 1 · Six story structures

Timings are shown as a share of runtime and as an example for a typical length. They're starting points: move beats if the footage demands it, but keep the order of *question → stakes → contact → complication → judgement → payoff → next step*.

### Structure 1: The Experiment Arc
*Series: 90 Minutes As… · Example runtime: 14 minutes*

| # | Beat | Share | Example time | What happens | Retention device |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open | 0–3% | 0:00–0:25 | The twist moment, raw | In medias res |
| 2 | Why this job | 3–10% | 0:25–1:25 | Host shows their real OffLadder three-question result and picks one of the two directions; meet the practitioner | Product as plot device; identity |
| 3 | The rules | 10–14% | 1:25–2:00 | The brief, the 90:00 clock, the Scorecard questions; **prediction prompt** | Verdict loop opened |
| 4 | First contact | 14–30% | 2:00–4:15 | The host starts; expectation against reality | Split-screen, fast cuts |
| 5 | First check-in | 30–38% | 4:15–5:20 | Practitioner judges progress and gives one tip | Expert judgement |
| 6 | The curveball | 38–50% | 5:20–7:00 | The planned disruption; the story catches up with the cold open | Midpoint reset |
| 7 | Recovery | 50–68% | 7:00–9:30 | The host adapts, fails a little and gets something working | Stakes ladder |
| 8 | The review | 68–78% | 9:30–11:00 | Practitioner reviews the output: *"What would you fix?"* | Judgement; tension |
| 9 | The real job | 78–84% | 11:00–11:45 | The part they'd delete; routes in; pay only on a sourced Evidence Card | Held-back reveal |
| 10 | Scorecard and Verdict | 84–92% | 11:45–12:55 | Three answers, then the stamp | Loop closed; silence before the stamp |
| 11 | Your Version and CTA | 92–98% | 12:55–13:45 | The viewer's experiment; OffLadder as the next step | Value before ask |
| 12 | Bridge | last 20 s | 13:45–14:05 | *"Next, we tried…"* over the end screen | Session continuation |

### Structure 2: The Tuesday Clock
*Series: Last Tuesday · Example runtime: 10 minutes*

| # | Beat | Example time | What happens | Retention device |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0:00–0:20 | Their exact "delete" answer as a quote, or a close-up of their real calendar | Specific and surprising |
| 2 | Frame | 0:20–1:00 | Who they are, and the premise: *"Not your best day. Last Tuesday, whatever it was."* | Honesty promise |
| 3 | The hours | 1:00–6:30 | On-screen clock ticks through the day. Each hour is a micro-story told with their phone photos, B-roll and short re-enactments | Clock progression; a surprise roughly every 90 s |
| 4 | The Tuesday afternoon | 6:30–7:15 | The texture of the slowest stretch: what it feels like, not just what happens | Emotional truth |
| 5 | The worst week | 7:15–8:30 | *"What does the worst week of your year look like?"* | Warning value |
| 6 | The delete answer | 8:30–9:15 | The full answer teased in the hook, with context | Loop closed |
| 7 | Your Version | 9:15–9:50 | Their suggestion for the smallest real task a viewer could try tonight | Value |
| 8 | Bridge | 9:50–10:10 | Next Last Tuesday episode, or the matching 90 Minutes As… | Session continuation |

### Structure 3: The Unknown Job
*Series: The Job Nobody Told You About · Example runtime: 11 minutes*

This mirrors the format of OffLadder's own "Jobs of the future" index, so the channel and the site describe directions the same way.

| # | Beat | Example time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open inside the work | 0:00–0:30 | The artefact or moment (the grown lamp, the stuck robot, the doctored photo) |
| 2 | What is this? | 0:30–1:15 | The direction in one sentence, using OffLadder's definition |
| 3 | What the days contain | 1:15–3:30 | Practitioner walkthrough, including the dull parts |
| 4 | Why it's appearing now | 3:30–4:45 | Sourced context on Evidence Cards only |
| 5 | What AI changes | 4:45–6:00 | Which tasks move to tools, and which need a person |
| 6 | The human abilities | 6:00–7:00 | Host attempts a mini version, and the viewer guesses along |
| 7 | What it grows out of | 7:00–8:30 | Routes in: *"Nobody starts here. People grow into it from…"* |
| 8 | What's genuinely unknown | 8:30–9:30 | **The honesty beat.** What nobody knows yet about this direction |
| 9 | One small test this week | 9:30–10:30 | Your Version |
| 10 | Bridge | 10:30–10:50 | The next unknown job |

### Structure 4: The Six-Week Arc
*Series: Change One Thing · Example runtime: 20 minutes*

The spine is OffLadder's parallel-track method: keep the income, give the new direction a few hours a week for six weeks, and decide with evidence.

| # | Beat | Example time | What happens | Retention device |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open | 0:00–0:40 | Week 6, the decision moment, unresolved | The big open loop |
| 2 | Who and why | 0:40–2:30 | The person, the job they want out of, and the constraint (mortgage, children, age) | Identity |
| 3 | The arithmetic | 2:30–4:00 | Monthly floor, months of runway, what the new direction plausibly pays in year one (from live adverts) | Realism; stakes |
| 4 | Week 1: inventory | 4:00–6:00 | "The three parts of my job I'm genuinely good at"; two adverts built from them in another industry | Discovery |
| 5 | Week 2: the conversation | 6:00–8:30 | Twenty minutes with someone doing the work: *"What's the boring part of the week?"* | New voice; a reveal |
| 6 | Weeks 3–4: the real piece | 8:30–13:00 | The smallest real piece of work for a real recipient, including **the low point** | Stakes ladder; vulnerability |
| 7 | Week 5: what pulled, what dragged | 13:00–15:30 | Their two-sentence notes; the pay from two live sources | Evidence |
| 8 | Week 6: the decision | 15:30–18:00 | More, variant, or off the table, with the written reason read aloud | The loop closes |
| 9 | Epilogue | 18:00–19:00 | Where they are some weeks later | Resolution |
| 10 | Your six weeks | 19:00–19:40 | The six-week template on screen; OffLadder as the tool for week one | Value, then CTA |
| 11 | Bridge | 19:40–20:00 | Next person's story | Session continuation |

### Structure 5: The Honest Answer
*Series: Honest Answers · Example runtime: 9 minutes*

| # | Beat | Example time | What happens | Retention device |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0:00–0:30 | The viewer's question in their words, plus the common wrong answer | Recognition, contrarian frame |
| 2 | The reframe | 0:30–2:00 | Why the usual advice fails, with one vivid example | "Oh" moment |
| 3 | Step 1 + pause beat | 2:00–3:30 | The first action, done by the viewer while watching | Participation |
| 4 | Step 2 + pause beat | 3:30–5:00 | Build on what they just wrote | Investment |
| 5 | Step 3 + example | 5:00–6:30 | A real person (or the host) goes through it | Proof |
| 6 | How to read the result | 6:30–7:30 | The questions that turn an experience into evidence | Payoff |
| 7 | This week | 7:30–8:15 | A plan specific enough to put in a calendar | Action |
| 8 | Product CTA | 8:15–8:45 | *"If you'd rather have the directions picked for you…"* | Value first, ask second |
| 9 | Bridge | 8:45–9:05 | The next logical video | Session continuation |

### Structure 6: The Shorts Loop
*Series: all Shorts · Runtime: 20–60 seconds*

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–1 s | Visual hook plus on-screen text (the question) |
| 1–3 s | Why it matters, in one line |
| 3 s to end − 5 s | Two to four fast beats (answers, examples, steps), each 5 seconds at most |
| Last ~5 s | Payoff or twist, written so the last line leads naturally back into the first frame (the loop) |
| On screen | Comment prompt as text, so saying it aloud doesn't break the loop |

## Part 2 · Retention technique library

Twenty-two techniques, grouped by what they do. The **Where** column says which series each one suits.

### A. Loops: give the viewer a question they need answered

| Technique | How to do it | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Verdict Loop** | Announce the Scorecard and the three possible verdicts in the first two minutes, and don't show the stamp until the last 15% | 90 Min, Change One Thing | *"At the end: more, variant, or off the table."* |
| **Prediction Prompt** | Ask viewers to commit to a guess in the comments *before* the outcome | 90 Min, Change One Thing, Shorts | *"Comment MORE, VARIANT or OFF THE TABLE before you see mine."* |
| **Held-back Reveal** | Tease the most interesting answer in the hook and pay it off in the last third | Last Tuesday | The "delete" answer |
| **Open-loop Stacking** | Open the next question before you close the current one, so there's never a natural exit point | All long-form | *"She got it working, but she hadn't noticed what was in the second email yet."* |
| **Chapter Questions** | Every chapter starts with a spoken question the chapter answers | All long-form | *"So what does a drone survey operator actually do all day?"* |

### B. Stakes: give the viewer a reason to care about the answer

| Technique | How to do it | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Clock** | Keep a visible countdown on screen and cut back to it at key moments | 90 Min | 90:00 → 43:10 → 00:00 |
| **The Planned Curveball** | Agree a realistic disruption with the practitioner in advance, and deliver it at 40–55% of runtime. The *host* must not know what or when | 90 Min | *"Your stage supplier has cancelled."* |
| **Expert Judgement** | A practitioner reviews the output on camera. Keep their honest critique in | 90 Min, Unknown Job | *"What would you fix?"* |
| **The Stakes Ladder** | Each segment raises the stakes: private → shown to an expert → delivered to a real recipient | 90 Min, Change One Thing | A plan → a reviewed plan → a plan a real organiser will use |
| **A Real Recipient** | The output goes to someone who'll use it, because real consequences make viewers care | Change One Thing | A real café gets the redesigned menu |

### C. Participation: the viewer does something

| Technique | How to do it | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pause Beats** | Ask the viewer to pause and write something down. Show a 10-second on-screen timer for people who don't pause, then continue | Honest Answers | *"Write down three things you lost track of time doing this year. I'll wait."* |
| **Guess-along** | Give the viewer the same challenge as the host, at the same moment | Unknown Job, Shorts | *"Real or fake? Decide now."* |
| **Score-along** | The viewer scores the host's performance or their own experiment using the Scorecard | 90 Min | *"Which twenty minutes looked fastest to you?"* |

### D. Rhythm: nothing in the way

| Technique | How to do it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual cadence** | A visual change every 2–4 seconds in the first minute; no static talking head for more than ~10 seconds anywhere, except in emotional moments where stillness is the point | All |
| **B-roll with a job** | Every cutaway shows something the words don't: the object, the mess, the detail. Never generic stock footage | All |
| **On-screen text for key lines** | The single sentence you want remembered goes on screen in the brand style | All |
| **Sound design** | Clock ticks under countdowns; **silence before every verdict**; no music fighting a voice | All |
| **Kill dead zones** | Apply the "so what?" test to every 30 seconds of the edit; anything that fails goes | All |

### E. Endings: pay off, then bridge

| Technique | How to do it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro-payoffs** | Something new every 45–90 seconds: a fact, a laugh, a surprise, a small win | All |
| **Callbacks** | The last line echoes the first. It feels complete, and viewers remember it | All |
| **Late CTA** | The product CTA comes *after* the payoff, never before the verdict | All |
| **Bridge ending** | The last 20 seconds give a specific reason to watch the next video. Never *"thanks for watching, see you next time"*, which viewers hear as permission to leave | All |

### Retention killers (search the rough cut for these)

- An intro that explains what the video will do instead of doing it.
- Setup before stakes. Viewers need a reason to care before they'll sit through context.
- Over-explaining a point the picture already made.
- A talking head with no visual change for more than 10 seconds.
- A CTA before the payoff.
- "So, yeah, that's it" signals before the real ending.
- Too many product mentions. **One integrated moment and one end CTA** is the ceiling.
- Music under dialogue that competes with the words.

## Part 3 · The retention edit (four passes)

1. **Story pass (assembly).** Does every scene serve the central question? Cut whole scenes before you trim lines.
2. **Tension pass.** Move reveals later, cut setup, open loops earlier, make sure the curveball lands at 40–55%.
3. **Rhythm pass.** Remove breaths, repetitions and false starts. Add B-roll, text or a punch-in to any static stretch over ~10 seconds.
4. **Clarity pass.** Captions (corrected, not raw auto-captions), a clean sound mix, Evidence Cards, chapter markers and brand graphics.

**Then do the stranger test.** Watch the whole cut on a phone at 1× speed, as if you've never seen it. Note every moment your thumb wants to scroll, and fix those first.

### Chapters: use them deliberately

YouTube chapters need a first timestamp of 00:00, at least three timestamps in ascending order, and chapters of at least 10 seconds ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)).

- **Honest Answers:** always use chapters. Search viewers value navigation, and descriptive chapters help the video match specific questions.
- **Experiments and documentaries:** use chapters with **non-spoiler titles** ("The review", "The verdict"), never "I hated it". If the retention graph shows people jumping straight to the verdict, test the next episode without chapters.

## Part 4 · Reading the retention graph

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **A cliff in the first 30 seconds** | The opening didn't confirm the click, or it started slowly | Re-cut the opening with the hook QA list. On a video that's already published, trim a slow start in the Studio editor |
| **A steady, gentle slope** | Normal. Flatter is better | Compare the slope with other videos in the same series |
| **A dip at a recurring segment type** (for example, every "why it's appearing now" section) | That segment type is weak | Shorten it, move it, or turn it into visuals in future episodes |
| **A spike** | People rewound or shared that moment | Cut it as a Short, and design future episodes to have more moments like it |
| **A drop 60–90 seconds before the end** | The video signalled "done" too early | Move the Scorecard later, and shorten everything after the verdict |
| **A drop at the CTA** | The CTA is too long, too early, or feels like an ad | Cut it to under 20 seconds and move it after the payoff |
| **A flat middle section (a "top moment")** | This is working | Reuse the structure of that section in the next script |

Compare videos **within a series** and of **similar length**. A 20-minute documentary and an 8-minute answer will never have the same shape.
