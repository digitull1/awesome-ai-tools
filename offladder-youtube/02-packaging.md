# 02 · Packaging: titles and thumbnails

> **The rule:** if we can't write a title and draw a thumbnail that *we* would tap over the video next to it, the video doesn't get made. The idea is the packaging.

Packaging decides whether anyone sees the work. Retention decides whether YouTube keeps showing it. This file covers the first half. The second half is in [04-story-and-retention.md](04-story-and-retention.md).

---

## 1. Packaging-first development

For every idea, **before a single line of script**:

1. Write **ten titles**. The first three will be obvious; the good ones usually come from seven to ten.
2. Sketch **three thumbnails** as rough boxes on paper or in Figma. Use three different archetypes (see §4), not three versions of one.
3. Score the best pair with **CLICK** (below).
4. Only ideas scoring **20/25 or higher, with K = 5**, go into production.

Every title must answer three questions in under two seconds: **What is it? Why should I care? Why this one rather than the ten others in my feed?**

---

## 2. The CLICK score

Score each factor from 1 to 5 for the best title and thumbnail pair.

| | Factor | The question | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | Curiosity | Is there a question the viewer can only answer by watching? | "Then the supplier cancelled" (what happened?) |
| **L** | Legibility | Does the thumbnail read in one second at phone size? | One face, one object, two words |
| **I** | Identity | Will the target viewer think "that's me" or "that's my question"? | "41, a mortgage, and done with her job" |
| **C** | Consequence | Is something at stake: time, money, a decision, a failure, a future? | A real person decides on camera at week six |
| **K** | Kept promise | Can the video deliver everything the packaging implies, with evidence? | The footage proves the title |

- **Greenlight:** 20+ out of 25 **and** K = 5. K is a veto, not a factor: a 24/25 idea with K = 4 gets rewritten or killed.
- **Worked example:** *I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes (Then the Supplier Cancelled)*. C5 · L4 · I3 · C5 · K5 = **22**. Identity is the weak spot, because only event-curious viewers self-select. We compensate in the hook by widening it to *"anyone who's ever organised anything."*

## 3. Title system

### 3.1 Rules

1. **Front-load.** The first ~50 characters must work alone because titles truncate on phones. Aim for 40–60 characters in total.
2. **Use the viewer's words.** Honest Answers titles use the exact phrase people search ("I don't know what career I want"). Experiment titles use story words ("then the supplier cancelled").
3. **One promise per title.** Two promises halve the curiosity.
4. **Specific beats general.** "90 Minutes" beats "a day". "The Supplier Cancelled" beats "It Went Wrong". "Last Tuesday" beats "a typical day".
5. **Complement the thumbnail; never repeat it.** The thumbnail shows, the title tells (see §5).
6. **Use a bracket for a second hook**, sparingly: *(Hour by Hour)*, *(Then the Supplier Cancelled)*, *(Try This First)*.
7. **Title Case, with no ALL-CAPS words.** The brand shouts in the thumbnail, not the title.
8. **The title must be true of the final edit.** Draft it before the shoot and lock it after the edit.

### 3.2 Banned patterns

These conflict with OffLadder's editorial rules, and several of them also cost trust:

- "AI-proof", "recession-proof", "future-proof" used as a promise. They're allowed only when the video challenges the idea.
- "Perfect career", "dream job", "the only advice you need", "guaranteed".
- Salary figures ("$150K jobs nobody wants").
- Numbers we can't show on screen ("99% of people get this wrong").
- Fake urgency ("before it's too late", "while you still can").
- "You won't believe…", "SHOCKING", or emoji used as bait.

### 3.3 Ten formulas, with OffLadder examples

| # | Formula | OffLadder examples | Why it clicks | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Test**: I Tried Being a [Job] for 90 Minutes (+ twist) | *I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes (Then the Supplier Cancelled)* · *I Had 90 Minutes to Make an AI Fail. ## 4. Thumbnail system

### 4.1 Brand tokens (taken from offladder.com)

| Token | Value | Use in thumbnails |
|---|---|---|
| Orange (accent) | `#FA5608` | The **one** accent per thumbnail: stamp, timer, highlight box |
| Ink | `#0C0C0C` | Text, hard shadows, outlines |
| Paper | `#F4F2EE` | Studio backdrop, badge fills |
| Paper light | `#FBFAF7` | Card fills |
| Muted | `#E8E6E0` / `#54524F` | Secondary tags only |
| Display type | **Archivo Black**, uppercase, tight tracking | All thumbnail words |
| Accent type | **Instrument Serif Italic** | Optional 1–3-word aside or quote, in orange, only when it still reads at small size |
| Shape | Square corners, hard **ink offset shadow** (the site's `8px 8px 0` at 1280 px wide) | Text boxes and badges, echoing the site's "CHOOSE ONE." hero box |

Using the site's visual language in thumbnails means that after about ten uploads the channel is recognisable in a feed before anyone reads a word, and the site feels familiar when viewers arrive.

### 4.2 Composition rules

1. **One subject, one idea, three elements at most** (for example, face + object + two words).
2. **The subject fills at least 40% of the frame height.** Faces carry emotion; objects carry the job.
3. **Four words at most, and ideally two.** The words must add something the title doesn't say.
4. **Keep the bottom-right corner clear**, because YouTube overlays the duration there. Keep ~5% margins everywhere.
5. **Real expressions from the shoot:** focus, doubt, "uh-oh", relief, laughter. No manufactured shock faces. A true micro-expression reads as honest, which is the brand.
6. **Eye line is a tool.** Looking at the camera builds connection (Honest Answers, Change One Thing). Looking at the object directs attention to the job (experiments).
7. **Background:** a real location, blurred, or a plain paper-coloured backdrop. Never busy.
8. **Orange is the only saturated colour you add.** Let the photo supply everything else.
9. **Size and format:** 1280×720 (16:9). Check Studio for the current file-size limit.

### 4.3 Series badges (top-left, small, always in the same place)

| Series | Badge |
|---|---|
| 90 Minutes As… | An orange block with **90:00** in ink, like a stopwatch readout. Twist variants show the time the twist hit (for example **43:10**) |
| Last Tuesday | A calendar tile: paper, orange top bar, **TUE** in ink |
| The Job Nobody Told You About | An ink index card: **JOB #07** |
| Change One Thing | A six-segment progress bar, filled to the week shown |
| Honest Answers | No badge. An orange highlight box behind one key word does the work |

### 4.4 Six thumbnail archetypes

**A · Mid-Task** (default for 90 Minutes As…)
Host in the middle of the work: hands on the job's object, eyes on the object, a real expression of concentration or doubt, and the 90:00 badge. Text: 0–2 words that add the twist ("NO STAGE.").
*Why it works:* the viewer sees a person mid-risk and wants to know how it ends.

**B · The Stamp** (contrarian topics, verdict reveals)
A document, photo or list with an orange rubber-stamp word across it: **GUESS**, **OFF THE TABLE**, **MORE**.
*Why it works:* a stamp is a judgement, and viewers click to see whether they agree. Test revealing the verdict ("OFF THE TABLE", so *why did they hate it?*) against hiding it.

**C · The Artefact** (The Job Nobody Told You About)
One striking, hero-lit object: a lamp grown from mushroom mycelium, a stuck delivery robot, a photo covered in forensic mark-up.
*Why it works:* an unfamiliar object creates the "what is that?" question without any words.

**D · Split Reality** (Last Tuesday, expectation against reality)
Left half, labelled **IMAGINED**: the glossy version of the job. Right half, labelled **TUESDAY**: the real, mundane moment (a spreadsheet at 4pm, a parents' evening queue).
*Why it works:* the gap between the two halves is the curiosity.

**E · Person + Clock** (Change One Thing)
A documentary portrait with the subject looking slightly away, the six-week progress bar, and optionally a four-word quote in Instrument Serif Italic ("what if I'm wrong?").
*Why it works:* a real face at a real decision point. Identity does the clicking.

**F · The Question** (Honest Answers)
The host looking into the lens, holding a single prop: a stack of personality-test printouts, a blank sheet, a phone showing an AI chat. One to three words in the orange box.
*Why it works:* direct eye contact plus a prop tells the viewer "this is for you, and it's practical".

### 4.5 The Thumbnail Minute (on every shoot)

Thumbnails are photographed, not found afterwards in frame grabs. On every shoot, block **60 seconds** for:

- The key prop in hand, from two angles (tight and medium).
- Three honest expressions (focus, doubt, relief), captured in burst mode.
- A clean background plate of the location with no people in it, for compositing text.
- The twist moment in burst mode as it happens. You can't recreate it later.

### 4.6 Thumbnail QA (before upload)

- [ ] **One-second test:** show it to someone for one second. Can they say what the video is about?
- [ ] **Small-size test:** view it at ~160 px wide. Can you still read the words and see the face?
- [ ] **Feed test:** drop it into a mock phone feed with 11 real thumbnails your target viewer would see (career change, day-in-the-life, productivity). Does it stand out? Ask five people from the target audience which one they'd tap.
- [ ] **Dark mode and light mode:** check it on both YouTube themes.
- [ ] **Truth test:** is everything in it true of the final edit?
- [ ] **Series badge** present and in the right place, and the bottom-right corner is clear.

## 5. Pairing titles and thumbnails

**The thumbnail shows. The title tells.** The thumbnail carries emotion and image, and the title carries context and stakes. Words on the thumbnail must add information the title doesn't have.

| Title | Thumbnail | Why the pair works |
|---|---|---|
| *I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes (Then the Supplier Cancelled)* | Archetype A: host on the phone, hand on forehead, a run-of-show taped to the wall, badge **43:10**, text **NO STAGE.** | The title says *something* was cancelled, and the thumbnail says *what*. Together: "the festival has no stage and there are 47 minutes left." |
| *Stop Asking Which Jobs AI Can't Replace* | Archetype B: a printed list headed "AI-PROOF JOBS" with an orange **GUESS** stamp, and the host's hand holding a pen | The title gives an instruction and the thumbnail gives the reason. Neither repeats the other. |
| *I Don't Know What Career I Want (Try This Before Anything Else)* | Archetype F: host at a table with one handwritten sheet, looking into the lens, text **NO IDEA? GOOD.** | The title matches the search, and the thumbnail flips the shame into permission, which is the emotional hook. |
| *41, a Mortgage, and Done With Her Job* | Archetype E: portrait of the subject at a kitchen table, progress bar at **WEEK 6**, quote *"what if I'm wrong?"* | The title gives the identity and constraint, and the thumbnail gives the moment of decision. |

## 6. Testing protocol

YouTube Studio's **Test & Compare** tests up to three titles, three thumbnails, or combinations of both. It runs from desktop Studio with advanced features enabled, and picks the winner by **watch time**, not click-through rate. Tests can take a few days or up to two weeks. They can't run on Shorts, on made-for-kids, mature-audience or private videos, or on Premieres and scheduled Lives until those convert ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)).

Because the winner is chosen by watch time, a misleading variant that wins clicks but loses viewers **loses the test**. The tool enforces our honesty rule for us.

**Our protocol:**

1. **Test every long-form upload.** Launch with three thumbnail variants built from *different archetypes*, not small tweaks. Big differences produce conclusive results; colour tweaks rarely do.
2. **After the first ten uploads, alternate.** Odd-numbered videos test thumbnails, and even-numbered videos test titles.
3. **Log every test** in the Packaging Log ([templates/packaging-log.md](templates/packaging-log.md)): variants, result, traffic mix, and one lesson.
4. **Write house rules from our own data.** After roughly ten conclusive tests, replace any guidance in this file that our data contradicts.
5. **"Performed the same" means keep the more on-brand variant.** "Inconclusive" means YouTube keeps the first upload, so upload the variant you believe in first.
6. **Manual changes without a test:** if CTR is well below our rolling median after 48–72 hours, with meaningful impressions from Browse, change the **thumbnail** first. Leave search-driven titles stable.

## 7. Packaging Shorts

- **The first frame is the thumbnail and the hook.** Put 2–6 words of on-screen text in the first frame, inside the safe area. Keep text out of the bottom ~20% and the right-hand ~15%, where the feed's buttons and captions sit.
- **Titles are conversational and short:** *"Would you delete this part of your job?"*, *"Your hobby is hiding three jobs"*.
- **Links in Shorts descriptions and comments aren't clickable** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639)). Use the Short's **related video** link to send viewers to the matching long-form video, and rely on the channel profile links.
- **Loop the ending** into the first frame where the format allows. See [04-story-and-retention.md](04-story-and-retention.md#structure-6-the-shorts-loop).
