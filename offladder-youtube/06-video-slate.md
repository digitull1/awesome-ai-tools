# 06 · Video slate: 22 long-form concepts and 24 Shorts

Every concept below has passed the CLICK score ([02-packaging.md](02-packaging.md#2-the-click-score)) at 20/25 or higher with a full 5 for *Kept promise*. Each card explains **why viewers would click** and **why they would keep watching**, so the team can judge the reasoning as well as the idea.

**How to read a card**

- **Titles:** A is the launch title, B and C are Test & Compare alternatives. Titles marked *(outcome-dependent)* are finalised after the edit.
- **Thumbnails:** archetype letters refer to [02-packaging.md §4.4](02-packaging.md#44-six-thumbnail-archetypes).
- **Hook numbers** refer to the archetypes in [03-hooks-and-openings.md](03-hooks-and-openings.md#3-twelve-hook-archetypes).
- **Structures** refer to [04-story-and-retention.md](04-story-and-retention.md#part-1--six-story-structures).
- **Risk and fact-check** lists what must be verified, permitted or protected before publishing.
- **People who haven't been cast yet.** Working titles and hooks that describe a specific person (for example *41, a Mortgage, and Done With Her Job*) are placeholders. Once someone is cast, rewrite them around that real person, including their age, situation and pronouns.

## Concepts with full scripts (01–07)

### 01 · I Don't Know What Career I Want

**Honest Answers** · 9–10 min · Students and the Quietly Stuck · Search, evergreen · Low cost · CLICK C4 L4 I5 C3 K5 = **21**

**Titles**
- A: I Don't Know What Career I Want (Try This Before Anything Else)
- B: No Idea What Career You Want? Do This, Not a Test
- C: How to Figure Out What Career You Want (Without Deciding Anything)

**Thumbnails**
- A (F · The Question): host at a kitchen table holding one handwritten sheet, looking into the lens. Orange box: **NO IDEA? GOOD.**
- B (Prop): close-up of the sheet, with three "lost track of time" lines and arrows to verbs. Text: **10 MINUTES**

**Hook:** The Pause (1). **Opening scene:** Blueprint 4, the prop on the table: a blank sheet and a pen.

**Structure:** The Honest Answer. The reframe (you're missing information, not ability) → why thinking about it doesn't work (the Tuesday afternoon) → Step 1: when did you last lose track of time? (pause beat) → Step 2: push each one down to a verb → Step 3: widen, with directions you've never heard of → Step 4: test three, cheaply → how to read the result → this week's plan → CTA → bridge.

**Why they click.** It uses the exact phrase people search. The thumbnail flips the shame of not knowing into permission, which is rare in a category that sells certainty. And "try this before anything else" promises a low-effort first move, not a lecture.

**Why they stay.** Within two minutes the viewer is writing about *their own life*, so the video becomes about them. Each later step transforms what they wrote, so leaving means abandoning something half-built. The verb examples ("arguing about football tactics → finding the pattern that explains why something works → sports analytics, epidemiology, fraud detection") land as mini-payoffs roughly every minute.

**CTA fit:** the strongest product CTA on the channel. The viewer has just found their verbs, and three questions turn them into two directions.

**Risk and fact-check:** no statistics needed. Avoid unsourced claims such as "most people can name about twenty jobs". Use the Viewer Challenge (hook 10) so the viewer produces that evidence themselves.

**Shorts spin-offs:** The Pause (skit) · Hobby → Verb: football tactics · 10 Jobs, 10 Seconds

### 02 · I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes

**90 Minutes As… (pilot)** · 13–15 min · Students and organisers of all ages · Browse · Medium cost · CLICK C5 L4 I3 C5 K5 = **22**

**Titles**
- A: I Tried Being a Live Event Producer for 90 Minutes (Then the Supplier Cancelled)
- B: I Had 90 Minutes to Plan a Festival. Then It Fell Apart.
- C: Can a Complete Beginner Plan a Festival in 90 Minutes?

**Thumbnails**
- A (A · Mid-Task): host on the phone, hand on forehead, run-of-show taped to the wall behind. Badge **43:10**, text **NO STAGE.**
- B (B · The Stamp): host holding the run-of-show with an orange **CANCELLED** stamp across the stage line.

**Hook:** The Clock (3), inside a cold open at the twist (4). **Opening scene:** Blueprint 2.

**Structure:** The Experiment Arc. The host's real three-question result → the brief: plan a one-day community festival → first contact with a run-of-show → first check-in → **curveball: the stage and sound supplier cancels** → re-plan → the practitioner's review → the part they'd delete → Scorecard → Verdict → Your Version.

**Why they click.** "I tried" plus a specific job plus a disaster promised in the bracket. The viewer anticipates chaos, feeling both empathy and a little schadenfreude. A festival is fun and familiar, and the clock turns it into a game.

**Why they stay.** The promised curveball is an open loop from second one. When it lands mid-video it resets attention, and the recovery gives the second half its own question. Almost everyone has organised *something*, so viewers score along. The verdict is withheld until the last 15%.

**CTA fit:** integrated. The host's real OffLadder result supplies the direction. **This only works if "live events" genuinely appears in the host's result.** If it doesn't, pilot with a direction that does.

**Risk and fact-check:** pay appears only as a sourced range on an Evidence Card. The practitioner signs off the realism of the scenario, and releases are signed.

**Shorts spin-offs:** The call (the twist) · What event producers would delete · Would you do it twice? (the repeat test)

### 03 · Stop Asking Which Jobs AI Can't Replace

**Honest Answers** · 9–11 min · The AI-Anxious (all ages) · Search ("AI-proof jobs", "jobs AI can't replace") and Browse · Low cost · CLICK C5 L5 I4 C4 K5 = **23**

**Titles**
- A: Stop Asking Which Jobs AI Can't Replace (Do This Instead)
- B: Every "AI-Proof Jobs" List Is a Guess. Here's What to Do.
- C: Which Jobs Are Safe From AI? Here's the Honest Answer *(replaces "Will AI Take My Job?…", which the research sprint found is dominated by a Channel 4 documentary of that name)*

**Thumbnails**
- A (B · The Stamp): a printed list headed **AI-PROOF JOBS** with an orange **GUESS** stamp, and the host's hand holding a pen.
- B (F · The Question): host holding two different printed lists side by side, one eyebrow raised. Text: **WHICH ONE?**

**Hook:** The Contrarian Truth (2). **Opening scene:** Blueprint 4. The host lays out published lists from different years and publishers and highlights where they disagree.

**Structure:** The Honest Answer. The lists, compared on camera → why they fail (they answer at the level of job titles when change happens at the level of tasks) → what seems to be holding value (ingredients, not titles) → the week audit (pause beat) → the 80% test, demonstrated by the host → the paragraph about the part of your work you'd least like a model to do → a plan for this month → CTA.

**Why they click.** It addresses a large, live anxiety with a frame that respects the viewer's intelligence. The stamp reads in a split second. People searching for AI-proof jobs get a surprising alternative *and* the reassurance that they aren't being fooled again.

**Why they stay.** The list comparison is a live reveal (*how much do they actually agree?*). Then the viewer audits their own week, and the host's 80% test produces a concrete "what I had to fix" list, which is the most tangible thing anyone has said to them about AI and their job.

**CTA fit:** test one adjacent direction now, *"so you're not learning about the market for the first time when you need it."*

**Risk and fact-check:** the list comparison must be real, counted on camera, with every list cited. Anything about what is changing slowly must be attributed to specific sources (WEF *Future of Jobs Report 2025*, ILO, OECD *Employment Outlook*) and checked against them.

**Shorts spin-offs:** Two lists, one question · The 20-minute paragraph · The 80% test

### 04 · I Asked ChatGPT to Choose My Career

**Honest Answers (live demo)** · 9–11 min · Students and the Quietly Stuck · Search and Browse · Low cost · CLICK C5 L4 I4 C3 K5 = **21**

**Titles** *(outcome-dependent)*
- A (if the framing effect appears): I Asked ChatGPT to Choose My Career. It Agreed With Everything.
- B (if it doesn't): I Asked ChatGPT to Choose My Career. Here's What It Can't Know.
- C: Don't Ask AI What Career to Do. Ask These 5 Questions Instead.

**Thumbnails**
- A: split screen of two chats with opposite verdicts (**real outputs only**), the host's puzzled face in the centre.
- B (F · The Question): host holding a phone showing an AI chat. Orange box: **IT AGREED.** *(only if true)*

**Hook:** Screen demo, *"Watch what happens when I change one word."* **Opening scene:** Blueprint 5.

**Structure:** The Honest Answer with live tests. Test 1, the framing test (two fresh chats, opposite framings) → Test 2, what it knows about you (only your description, never what you actually did) → what assistants are genuinely good at → five prompts that produce something checkable → the host runs prompt 3 and does the one-hour task it suggests → CTA.

**Why they click.** AI plus careers is a huge overlap, and many viewers have already tried this themselves. A promised failure mode is irresistible, and title C serves search with a practical promise.

**Why they stay.** The A/B framing reveal is a strong mid-video payoff. The five prompts are copyable, so people stay to screenshot them. The host doing the one-hour task ends on action, not opinion.

**CTA fit:** honest differentiation. OffLadder keeps a record of what you tried and ends in something to do. We name what general assistants are good at, too.

**Risk and fact-check:** screen-record everything. State the model, version and date on screen. Run each test three times and report the pattern honestly, with no cherry-picking. Use the product name only descriptively.

**Shorts spin-offs:** Change one word · Ask It Better ×5 (one prompt per Short)

### 05 · Is This Photo Real? Meet the People Paid to Find Out

**The Job Nobody Told You About** · 11–12 min · Students and the AI-Anxious · Browse · Medium cost · CLICK C5 L5 I3 C4 K5 = **22**

**Titles**
- A: Is This Photo Real? Meet the People Paid to Find Out
- B: Real or Fake? This Is Someone's Whole Job
- C: The Job of Proving Where a Photo Came From

**Thumbnails**
- A (C · The Artefact): a photo covered in verification mark-up (circled shadows, arrows, a timestamp). Orange box: **REAL?** Badge **JOB #05**.
- B: the same scene twice, labelled **REAL** and **AI**, with a question mark between them. *(Labelled as synthetic in-video.)*

**Hook:** Real or Fake? (8). **Opening scene:** Blueprint 1. The photo fills the screen and a three-second timer runs.

**Structure:** The Unknown Job. Guess rounds → meet a verification practitioner → what the days contain → why it's appearing now → what AI changes → the human abilities → what it grows out of → what's genuinely unknown → one small test.

**Why they click.** It combines a live anxiety (*can I trust what I see?*), a game (*real or fake?*) and a novelty (*someone is paid to do this?*).

**Why they stay.** The guess-along rounds give the viewer a stake every minute. Watching an expert's method beat your instinct is compelling. The honesty beat (*nobody knows yet whether detection can keep up*) closes with weight rather than hype.

**CTA fit:** *"Provenance work is one of the directions OffLadder can point people towards"*, but only if the product's direction library includes it.

**Risk and fact-check:** label every synthetic image on screen and use YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure. Don't teach evasion techniques. Never fake a real person's likeness. Use only images we own or have rights to. Get the practitioner's employer's permission.

**Shorts spin-offs:** Real or AI? (answer at the end) · The first thing a verifier checks · Real job or made up?

### 06 · 41, a Mortgage, and Done With Her Job

**Change One Thing** · 18–22 min · The Quietly Stuck · Suggested and Browse · High cost (six weeks) · CLICK C4 L4 I5 C5 K5 = **23**

**Titles**
- A: 41, a Mortgage, and Done With Her Job
- B: She Tested a New Career in 6 Weeks Without Quitting
- C: Changing Careers at 41 Without Blowing Up Her Life

**Thumbnails**
- A (E · Person + Clock): portrait at a kitchen table, looking away, progress bar at **WEEK 6**, quote *"what if I'm wrong?"* (must be her own words).
- B: split between her two worlds, today's laptop and lanyard against the new direction's tool, with the progress bar.

**Hook:** The Call-Out (9), inside a cold open on the week-6 decision (4). **Opening scene:** Blueprint 2.

**Structure:** The Six-Week Arc. Who and why → the arithmetic (floor, runway, year-one pay) → week 1 inventory → week 2 conversation → weeks 3–4 real piece, including the low point → week 5 notes and pay research → week 6 decision → epilogue → the viewer's six weeks.

**Why they click.** Identity plus constraint signals realism, not fantasy. "Without quitting" answers this audience's biggest fear before they have to voice it.

**Why they stay.** A real decision with real stakes is withheld until the end. The arithmetic segment is almost never shown on YouTube and is exactly what this audience lies awake calculating. The low point creates empathy, and empathy holds viewers.

**CTA fit:** the six-week template, OffLadder for week one, and the casting call.

**Risk and fact-check:** written consent at the start *and* before publishing. Her employer must not be identifiable unless they agree, and being filmed must never put her job at risk. Financial details are shared only with her explicit consent. Pay data comes from live adverts, cited. Include a wellbeing check-in at every shoot.

**Shorts spin-offs:** The arithmetic · "What's the boring part of the week?" · What would you decide?

### 08 · I Had 90 Minutes to Make an AI Fail

**90 Minutes As…** · 13–15 min · The AI-Anxious, Students · Browse and Search ("AI jobs") · Medium cost · CLICK C5 L4 I4 C4 K5 = **22**
*Direction: model evaluation writer, "designing the tests that show whether an AI system is good enough for a specific real use, and writing down honestly where it fails."*

**Titles**
- A: I Had 90 Minutes to Make an AI Fail. It's a Real Job.
- B: Testing AI for a Living: I Tried It for 90 Minutes
- C: 90 Minutes as an AI Tester (I Thought This Would Be Easy)

**Thumbnails**
- A (A · Mid-Task): host leaning into a laptop with a grin of mischief. The screen shows a chat bubble with a big orange ✗. Badge **90:00**, text **FIND THE FAIL**.
- B (B · The Stamp): a printout of an AI answer with pen corrections and an orange **WRONG** stamp.

**Hook:** *"I have ninety minutes to find out where an AI gets things wrong, before anyone relies on it. That's a real job, and the person teaching me is very good at it."*
**Opening scene:** Blueprint 5. A real failure observed during testing, shown on screen.

**Structure:** The Experiment Arc. **The brief:** a café wants an AI to answer customers' questions about its menu. Is it good enough? Write ten test questions (easy, tricky, edge cases) → run them → grade them against a rubric → **curveball:** the practitioner reveals the menu changed yesterday, so does the AI know? → write up "good enough for X, not for Y" → review → Scorecard → Verdict.
**Your Version:** pick a topic you know extremely well (your hobby). Ask an AI five hard questions about it. Mark each answer right, wrong or *confidently wrong*, and write down the pattern.

**Why they click.** AI topicality, plus a playful and slightly mischievous mission. "It's a real job" adds hidden-job novelty. And viewers want to know whether a normal person can out-think an AI.

**Why they stay.** Every test question is a mini-reveal (*will it fail?*). The rubric makes the viewer a judge, the curveball raises the stakes, and watching confident machines get things wrong is enduringly satisfying.

**CTA fit:** integrated, *if* the host's result genuinely included this direction.

**Risk and fact-check:** no jailbreaking or harmful prompting, because this is quality testing, not attack. Show real outputs, and state the model, version and date. On screen: *"Don't rely on AI for allergy or medical advice."* Show real job adverts for evaluation roles, with permission or with company names blurred.

**Shorts spin-offs:** Confidently wrong (the best failure) · Grade this answer (the viewer judges)
