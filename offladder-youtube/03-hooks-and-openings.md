# 03 · Hooks and opening scenes

> The thumbnail made a promise. The first 30 seconds either keep it or break it.

YouTube Studio's retention report labels the **Intro** as the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)). It is usually the steepest drop on the curve and the cheapest part of a video to improve. Treat it as its own deliverable: script it, shoot it and edit it separately.

---

## 1. The four jobs of the first 30 seconds

| Time | Job | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **0–5 s** | **Confirm the click.** The first frame and line pay off the title and thumbnail immediately. If the thumbnail shows a drone, the first shot is the drone. | *"This is a drone. In ninety minutes I need to use it to tell a farmer something they don't know about their own field."* |
| **5–15 s** | **Open the loop.** Name the question the video will answer, specifically enough that it itches. | *"Can I actually do this? And more to the point: would I want to do it twice?"* |
| **15–25 s** | **Raise the stakes.** Say why it matters, whether to the host, the subject or the viewer. | *"Because if I like it, it's a real direction, and if I hate it, I've saved myself a two-year course."* |
| **25–30 s** | **Promise the path.** Give a reason to stay until the end: the verdict, the scorecard, the viewer's own experiment. | *"At the end I'll score it with three questions, and give you a version you can try this weekend."* |

---

## 2. Hook rules

1. **Start at the most interesting moment**, not at the beginning.
2. **No greeting, no channel intro, no logo sting before 0:30.** A one-second series sting after the hook is the maximum.
3. **Keep the first sentence to 12 words or fewer**, and make it understandable with zero context.
4. **Use the viewer's own words** for their problem ("I don't know what career I want"), not ours ("career exploration").
5. **Show before you tell.** A shot of the stuck robot beats a sentence about robots.
6. **Don't ask for anything in the first minute.** No subscribe, no like, no link.
7. **Promise only what you pay off.** Fake stakes in the hook show up as a dip in the retention graph.
8. **Change the visual every 2–4 seconds in the first 30** (a cut, punch-in, B-roll, text or prop), then relax the pace for emotional scenes.
9. **Put the question on screen as text** when it's the core of the video. Phone viewers in noisy places still get it.
10. **Read it aloud.** If it sounds like a movie trailer, rewrite it until it sounds like a person talking to one friend.

## 3. Twelve hook archetypes

Each archetype has a ready-to-use OffLadder line. Lines marked **[HOST: real]** must be replaced with the host's true experience, and lines marked **[FACT-CHECK]** need a source before recording.

### 1 · The Pause (the emotional mirror)
> *"Someone asks what you want to do. You say you're not sure. And then there's this pause, and in that pause you can feel them deciding you haven't got it together. I want to talk about that pause, because it's wrong."*

- **Use for:** Honest Answers for students and the quietly stuck.
- **Why it works:** it describes a private feeling so precisely that the viewer thinks "they get me". Trust is earned in five seconds, before any advice.

### 2 · The Contrarian Truth
> *"Every list of AI-proof jobs you've ever read is a guess. I'll show you why in about ninety seconds, and then give you something better than a list."*

- **Use for:** AI topics and contrarian Honest Answers.
- **Why it works:** it challenges a belief the viewer holds, promises proof, and promises a replacement. Criticism alone doesn't hold attention, but criticism plus an alternative does.

### 3 · The Clock
> *"I've got ninety minutes to plan a one-day festival. At some point in the next hour, somebody is going to cancel on me. I don't know who, and I don't know when."*

- **Use for:** 90 Minutes As…
- **Why it works:** a hard constraint plus an unknown threat produces suspense. The viewer stays to see the threat arrive.

### 4 · In Medias Res (cold open at the twist)
> *[Phone to ear.] "…Sorry, the whole stage? Both of them?" [Beat. Host lowers the phone.] [Series sting, 1 s.] "Forty minutes earlier, this was going really well."*

- **Use for:** experiments and documentaries.
- **Why it works:** it opens at the peak, so the viewer needs the backstory and stays for it.

### 5 · The Embarrassing Question
> *"Is thirty too late to change careers? If you've typed that into a search bar at one in the morning, this is for you, and the answer is more useful than just 'no'."*

- **Use for:** search-led Honest Answers.
- **Why it works:** it repeats the searcher's exact words, removes the shame of having searched them, and promises more than a platitude.

### 6 · The Live Experiment Promise
> *"By the end of this video you'll have run your first career experiment. It takes about ten minutes, you need a pen, and you don't need to know what you want."*

- **Use for:** Honest Answers.
- **Why it works:** a concrete deliverable for low effort, with the main barrier ("I don't know what I want") removed in advance.

### 7 · The Absurd-but-True Job
> *"Somewhere right now, a robot carrying supplies down a hospital corridor is stuck. And getting it moving again is somebody's job."* **[FACT-CHECK with the practitioner]**

- **Use for:** The Job Nobody Told You About.
- **Why it works:** a vivid image plus "wait, that's a job?" Novelty is one of the strongest click drivers, and the specificity makes it believable.

### 8 · Real or Fake?
> *[A photo fills the screen.] "Real or fake? Three seconds. [Beat.] I said real. I was wrong. The person you're about to meet worked it out in under a minute, and that's their job."* **[HOST: real]**

- **Use for:** the provenance episode, and any job with a skill the viewer can attempt.
- **Why it works:** instant participation, a small failure, then an expert. The viewer wants to learn the method that beat them.

### 9 · The Call-Out
> *"If you're forty-ish, good at your job, and quietly certain you don't want twenty more years of it, and you've got a mortgage so you can't just quit, stay with me for fifteen minutes."*

- **Use for:** Change One Thing, and Honest Answers for 30, 40 and 50.
- **Why it works:** the identity match comes with the constraint acknowledged. The viewer hears "finally, someone being realistic."

### 10 · The Viewer Challenge
> *"Quick test. Name as many jobs as you can in ten seconds. Go. [On-screen timer.] … Now: how many of those have you actually seen someone do, up close? That's the list you're choosing your future from."*

- **Use for:** Honest Answers and Shorts.
- **Why it works:** the viewer generates their own evidence, so the point lands without us claiming a statistic.

### 11 · The Cost of Guessing
> *"It's possible to spend two years deciding on a career, commit to it, and then find out in six weeks what one afternoon would have told you."*

- **Use for:** Honest Answers.
- **Why it works:** loss aversion, framed as avoidable. It's written as a possibility, not a statistic, so it stays true.

### 12 · The Confession
> *"I've taken four career tests. They told me I should be an accountant, a counsellor, a park ranger and, my favourite, 'an innovator'. Not one of them told me what any of those jobs feel like on a Tuesday."* **[HOST: real]**

- **Use for:** the career-test Honest Answer and teen videos.
- **Why it works:** vulnerability plus humour plus a near-universal experience. The punchline carries the thesis.

## 4. Opening-scene blueprints

Five openings, each with a shot list for the first 30 seconds. Match the blueprint to the series.

### Blueprint 1 · Match cut from the thumbnail
*Best for: experiments, The Job Nobody Told You About.*

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | The thumbnail moment, live: same prop, same angle, same expression, then the action continues | Location sound, no music yet |
| 0:03–0:10 | Punch in on the object, cut to the host's face | Hook line (archetype 3 or 7) |
| 0:10–0:22 | Two or three B-roll shots of the job in action; on-screen text poses the question | The loop and the stakes |
| 0:22–0:30 | Host to camera; the Scorecard graphic flashes past | The promise ("three questions at the end…"); music enters |

### Blueprint 2 · Cold open at the twist
*Best for: 90 Minutes As…, Change One Thing.*

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | The twist, raw and handheld: the phone call, the failed test, the tears, the laugh | Real dialogue, no score |
| 0:08–0:09 | Series sting (1 second at most) | Sting |
| 0:09–0:15 | Clock rewinds on screen: "43 MINUTES EARLIER" | Clock tick |
| 0:15–0:30 | The calm before: the setup and the rules, cut fast | Host VO sets the stakes and promises the verdict |

### Blueprint 3 · The street question
*Best for: Honest Answers openings, Shorts, and the Delete One Part series.*

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | The question on screen as text: "WHICH PART OF YOUR JOB WOULD YOU DELETE?" | Host asks it off-camera |
| 0:02–0:20 | Four to six rapid answers from real people (released), each 2–4 seconds | Their answers |
| 0:20–0:30 | Host turns to camera | *"Every one of those answers tells you more than their job title would. Here's how to use that."* |

### Blueprint 4 · The prop on the table
*Best for: Honest Answers.*

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Host at a table with a prop representing the topic: a stack of test results, a printed "AI-proof" list, a blank calendar | Hook line |
| 0:04–0:15 | The prop gets used physically: stamped, torn, circled, turned over | The contrarian point |
| 0:15–0:30 | Host looks up at the lens | The live experiment promise (archetype 6) |

### Blueprint 5 · The screen demo
*Best for: AI topics and data experiments.*

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Screen recording: an AI assistant typing out a confident answer | Host VO: *"Watch what happens when I change one word."* |
| 0:06–0:14 | Split screen: two chats, two framings, two opposite recommendations | Silence, letting the viewer read |
| 0:14–0:30 | Host to camera | The stakes and the promise: *"Here's what that means for you, and the five questions that actually work."* |

## 5. Hook bank

These are ready to adapt. Keep the bracket notes: they are the checks that keep hooks true.

### 90 Minutes As…
1. *"Game writers have to make you care about something in three lines. I've got ninety minutes to make you care about a rock."*
2. *"This is a drone. In ninety minutes I need to use it to tell a farmer something they don't already know about their own field."*
3. *"My flat is always cold. Today the person whose job is to find out why is letting me try their job on my own flat."*
4. *"I have ninety minutes to make an AI fail on purpose. It turns out that's a job, and the people who do it are very good at it."*
5. *"Vet nurses kept telling me the hardest part of the job isn't the animals. I've got ninety minutes to find out what it is."* **[VERIFY in pre-interviews]**

### Last Tuesday
6. *"This is a teacher's calendar from last Tuesday. Look at 3:40pm."*
7. *"'The forty minutes in the hospital car park.' That's the part one paramedic said they'd delete from their job."* **[Template: use the practitioner's real answer, word for word]**
8. *"Day-in-the-life videos show you the best day. We asked for last Tuesday, whatever it was."*
9. *"Everyone asks what the best part of the job is. We asked about the worst week of the year."*

### The Job Nobody Told You About
10. *"This lamp was grown, not made. It started as [substrate] and a fungus, and someone could do this for a living."* **[FACT-CHECK]**
11. *"The internet is built for people with a mouse. For the next ten minutes, I'm not allowed to use one."*
12. *"When a heatwave hits a city, somebody has to decide who gets checked on first. That person has a job title, and you've probably never heard it."*
13. *"Real or fake? You have three seconds."*
14. *"Most of the electricity question isn't how much we use. It's when. And somebody gets paid to move 'when'."* **[FACT-CHECK with practitioner]**

### Change One Thing
15. *"Six weeks ago, [Name] said: 'I want out.' Today is the decision. [Name] doesn't know which way it's going to go, and neither do we."*
16. *"[Name] is thirty and convinced it's too late. Six weeks, four hours a week, and quitting isn't allowed."*
17. *"Eight years without a job title. About forty jobs."* **[Template: use the subject's real list from the week-1 inventory]**

### Honest Answers
18. *"Before you take another career test, do this with the last one you took."*
19. *"'Should I learn coding, data, or something else?' Wrong question. Here's the right one."*
20. *"You don't need a career plan. You need a Tuesday."*
21. *"Changing careers at forty isn't a reinvention. It's a redeployment. Here's the difference, and why it matters for your mortgage."*
22. *"There's one question that tells you more about a job than an hour of 'so what's it like?' It's this: which part would you delete?"*
23. *"Your hobby isn't a job. But the verb underneath it might be three."*
24. *"If a personality test told you to be an accountant and your stomach dropped, that feeling is data. Let's use it."*

### Shorts (first-frame text + first line)
25. **"WHICH PART WOULD YOU DELETE?"** *"Which part of your job would you delete if you could?"*
26. **"YOUR HOBBY = 3 JOBS"** *"Tell me your hobby and I'll find three jobs hiding in it."*
27. **"10 JOBS. 10 SECONDS."** *"Name ten jobs. Ten seconds. Go."*
28. **"REAL OR AI?"** *"Real or AI? Decide before the end."*
29. **"DON'T ASK AI THIS"** *"Stop asking AI what career you should do. Ask this."*
30. **"THE PAUSE."** *[Skit] "So… what do you want to do?"*
31. **"NOVELTY LIES."** *"Everything's fun the first time. Here's the test that isn't fooled."*
32. **"ASK ABOUT THE WORST WEEK"** *"Don't ask what someone loves about their job. Ask about the worst week of their year."*

## 6. Hook QA checklist

Run this on the script **and** again on the rough cut.

- [ ] The first frame matches the thumbnail's promise.
- [ ] The first line is 12 words or fewer and makes sense with zero context.
- [ ] The core question is stated, out loud or on screen, by **0:15**.
- [ ] The stakes are clear by **0:25**.
- [ ] A reason to stay until the end (verdict, scorecard, Your Version) is given by **0:30**.
- [ ] Nothing in the first 30 seconds is setup the viewer doesn't need yet.
- [ ] No greeting, no subscribe request, no logo sting over one second.
- [ ] The visual changes every 2–4 seconds.
- [ ] Every claim is true and sourced, and every **[HOST: real]** line is real.
- [ ] Read aloud, it sounds like one person talking to one friend.
